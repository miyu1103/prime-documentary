## The one thing that changes the film

The handover brief said Hawaiʻi's siren network "did not sound." The state's own investigation says
something more specific, and the difference is the episode:

> **Finding 37: "Only one (1) siren from the All-Hazard Outdoor Warning Siren System was operable
> within the burn perimeter of the Lahaina area on August 8, 2023."**
> **Finding 38: "The All-Hazard Outdoor Warning Siren System had not been utilized for warning of
> WUI fires prior to August 8, 2023."**

Both are read from FSRI's Phase Two findings appendix, hosted by the Hawaiʻi AG, not from a news
summary. Add the reported detail that Maui's four Lahaina-area sirens are all sited by the ocean —
a network built for a wave arriving from the sea, in a town that burned from the mountain side
down. That is the controlling idea and it is specific enough that it cannot be mistaken for the
conspiracy material this topic sits beside, which was trap 1 in the brief.

## Two received facts the primary record contradicts

1. **"A power outage disabled the water pumps."** Finding 21: no pumps are used outside the
   production facilities, and "both systems had uninterrupted electrical power during the August
   2023 fires and produced water at capacity for the duration of the fire." The pressure loss is
   Finding 24 — burned structures' plumbing bleeding the system. `⛔-08` forbids the received version.
2. **"How it rekindled is undetermined."** The County's own release says the embers "remained
   undetected and were rekindled by a severe wind event at approximately 2:52 p.m." Only the
   *classification* alternatives include Undetermined; the fire is classified **Accidental**.

Also worth knowing: the utility gave its electrical data **only to ATF**, under a nondisclosure
agreement, and the Maui Fire Department was not granted access to it — in the County's own words.
