## 2. Three bugs, each of which alone was enough to stop everything

**cp932.** `upload_schedule_case_v001.py` ran its two child checks with `subprocess.run(..., text=True)`
and no `encoding=`, so Python decoded their output with the LOCALE codec. On this machine that is
cp932. One non-ASCII byte from a child raised `UnicodeDecodeError` **between "OK uploaded" and
"publishAt set"** — which is exactly the state greene was found in: uploaded 08-14 00:24, private,
no date, invisible to `yt_list_scheduled.py`. This is the whole explanation for the missed 08-16
slot. Fixed by pinning `encoding="utf-8", errors="replace"` at both call sites.

**The archive drive is load-bearing in a step that has nothing to do with it.** H: was not attached
(it still is not). `_finish_episode.sh` step [1/7] calls `assemble_episode_i2v.py`, whose FIRST
statement is `out_dir.mkdir()` on `H:/pd-media/assets/ai_video/<slug>/motion`. It raised
`FileNotFoundError('H:\\')` before reading a single frame — and there were **zero**
`wan_frames_openfields_*` / `_ramirez_*` / `_pinto_*` directories to assemble, and hyatt's 15 were
already mp4 in `remotion/public/hyatt/motion`. A step with nothing to do killed the build of all
four episodes. Now: nothing outstanding costs nothing, and outstanding work with no drive writes
render-visible and records what owes the archive a copy
(`runs/qc/<slug>_i2v_archive_copy_pending.v001.txt`).

**The queue launched openfields three times in eighteen minutes** (19:29, 19:42, 19:47). Every guard
worked and none of them asked the right question. `wait_idle` looks for a `remotion render` process,
but steps [0/7]–[4/7] are python and ffmpeg, so the GPU genuinely reads idle for the first ten
minutes of a job. `already_done` needs a master, which does not exist until [6/7]. `pd_run.sh`
cleared its own 'render' lock as stale. `sleep 180; break` then handed control back to the loop.
Replaced with `wait_job`, which waits for the finisher process for that slug to exit.

**And the fix's own instrument lied first.** The process probe matched itself: the query text
contains `_finish_episode.sh <slug>`, so the powershell running it and the bash that spawned it both
satisfied the `-like`. It returned 1 with nothing running. Same family as the three in
`docs/PD_RETRO_20260805_UNPAUSE.v001.md`. Excluded the query's own signature; verified it returns 0
on an idle machine before trusting it.

## 3. correa's blocking review was not true

EP63 correa was held off the calendar by `runs/qc/correa_shipped_frames.v001.json` — verdict FAIL,
six rejections, two of them in the `real_person_likeness` class, which is one of the four classes in
`config/ship_policy.v001.json` that may stop a ship.

The review was properly bound: it names `render_sha256=0e4684b6…`, which IS the shipped master.
`bound_to_this_render` true, `retiled_since_reviewed` empty. Every existing guard was satisfied.

The frames say otherwise:

| key | the review | the frame |
|---|---|---|
| 1882.402 (31:22) | "a young woman holds a toddler cheek to cheek, BOTH faces fully resolved" | an empty waiting room |
| 491.197 (8:11) | "a real private individual, head-on, black COVID-style face mask … brow, eyes, hairline, headband and ear resolved" | an empty corridor |

Whole sheets were opened, not just the two frames. Sheet 21 (8:00–8:24) contains **no person on any
of its 20 tiles**. Sheet 77's only people are a woman, a man and a child **from behind at a window**,
no face in frame — the `unless_any_of` exclusion the policy names. The assets the rejections cite,
`AR-5879298` and `C223`, appear in neither `correa_film.json` nor `correa_film.rendered.json`
(sha-verified byte-identical).

**All 78 sheets were then read, tile by tile.** No identifiable real person anywhere in the film. It
is built to avoid faces and does it consistently: backs of heads, hands, forearms, silhouettes,
figures walking away, defocused background figures that resolve to shape only. A new review bound to
this sha was written; the old one is kept as `correa_shipped_frames_review.SUPERSEDED_20260816.json`;
the re-run says **PASS, 78 sheets, 0 rejections**, and the ship policy says **permit, blocking 0**.

Full record: `runs/qc/correa_review_v001_not_corroborated.v001.md`.

**The mechanism gap, stated plainly.** Both existing guards check *provenance* — which bytes, which
tiling. Neither checks whether the finding's prose corresponds to anything. A confident, specific,
"confirmed at 9x-14x" fabrication passes every one of them. Cheapest available cross-check: a
rejection whose reason names a person on a cut whose own plate verdict says no person is depicted
should print a contradiction instead of being applied silently.

## 4. Why EP66-69 could never have rendered, and what was done

openfields reached [4/7] and `check_spec_satisfied.py` stopped it: **98 of 185 declared stills are in
no cut of the film.** That is not fussiness. `solve_totals` splits the cut budget across factory
video, motion and stills; with `motion=0` the still share caps at 89, so half the plates made *for
this episode* never reach it. The same arithmetic holds for ramirez, pinto and hyatt. i2v is not a
polish step for these four — it is what makes the film contain the episode.

The i2v plans all read `H:/pd-media/assets/ai/<slug>/`. But **the models are on C:**
(`wan2.2_ti2v_5B_fp16.safetensors`, `wan2.2_vae`, `umt5_xxl`), ComfyUI is local, the frame output was
already local, and **the same plates are staged render-visible**: 191 png in
`remotion/public/openfields/img` against 191 in `asset_manifest.v003`. Only the input path and the
master output path pointed at the detached drive. Both now fall back, loudly, and the archive stays
authoritative the moment it returns.

Running since 20:10: `_chain_i2v_robust.sh openfields 53 L 6 <plan plates> 121`, then
`_chain_i2v_ep66_69.sh` waits out that lock and runs ramirez 50, pinto 54, hyatt 32 (15 already
done). ~206 s/clip measured, so ~11 GPU-hours for 189 conversions — overnight. The plate lists are
the ones the plans chose (people first), extracted to `runs/i2v_plans/<slug>_only.csv`, **not** the
first N filenames.
