## 2. The erosion half of the quarantine decision is closed

09-07 stopped `search_archive.py` **serving** the eleven thrown-out themes, and recorded that the
ingest would keep **refilling** them. That half is now done (`f9842a61`).

It belongs in the ingest more than anywhere else, because **the ingest is where the rot comes
from**: the stock sources match a query a word at a time ("judge bench gavel" → "bank wooden
**bench** relax sea") and whatever comes back is filed under the theme that asked. Continuing to
download into a theme a person threw out re-creates exactly what they threw out.

```
--theme all:  46 themes -> 38   (8 of the 11 are ingest themes; the other 3 are filename-derived)
skipped: americana_1930s_1970s, anonymous_crowd, courtroom_justice, economy_crisis,
         household_loss, laboratory_forensics, police_modern, small_town
```

Demonstrated three directions before being relied on: skipped under `--theme all` with every name
printed; **not** skipped with `--include-quarantined-themes`; still skipped when named explicitly,
with the override named in the message. The running task was not restarted — the change takes
effect at its next 03:07 launch, the same way the download fix did.

**`recover_stock_shelf.py` needs no change, and the 09-07 note that said it did was wrong.** It
re-derives a theme when it re-links a file, but the guard is at the serving layer: `search_archive`
refuses the theme *name* regardless of how the row got there. Restoring a file cannot create a leak
the serving guard does not already close.

## 3. Four videos that were not in the semantic index, and only one of them was a defect

The clip index was missing exactly four files. They looked like corruption. They were three
different things:

* **Two are `Transformers Animated: The Battle Begins` DVD ISO rips** — a commercial Hasbro/Cartoon
  Network disc, sitting on the shelf under `vintage_ads_cartoons`. **Already `RIGHTS HOLD`**
  (`ia`, review_required), so the rights gate held on its own and nothing needs doing. Same family
  as the Blu-ray remux and the McDonald's advert found on 08-27.
* **Two are perfectly good clips**, 1920×1080 h264, ffprobe clean — and **1.001 s and 1.017 s
  long**. `grab_frame` samples at 1.2 s, 3.5 s and 7.0 s. Every one of those offsets is past the end
  of the file, so **no clip shorter than 1.2 seconds could ever enter the semantic index.** They
  were not broken; the sampler could not reach them.

`"0.0"` is now a fourth, fallback offset. Both clips yield a frame, and **the clip index is
complete for the first time: 31,459 of 31,459** (it had sat at 31,455). The blind spot was two
files today, which is small — but it was structural, and it would have grown with every short clip
the shelf ever gained.

Worth keeping: a file absent from an index reads as damage. Two of these four were rights-blocked
commercial video, one class of problem; two were a sampler that could not reach the end of a short
file, a completely different one. **"Missing from the index" is a symptom with several diseases.**
