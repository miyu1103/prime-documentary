On the night of 29 October 2022, the first Halloween in three years without distancing rules, 159 people died in a street in Itaewon, Seoul. The official figure, recorded by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, is 159. A further hundred and ninety-six people were injured. Twenty-six of the dead were foreign nationals, from fourteen countries.

The mechanism is on the record. In 2024 four researchers published a study of that night in PLOS One, reconstructing the crowd from the record. They put the average density during the crush at 7.57 people per square metre, with a maximum of 9.95, and recorded that over 300 people were concentrated in an area of eighteen square metres. The primary causes, in their words, were the substantial population, bidirectional collision, and escalating panic — a bidirectional stream of pedestrians colliding in a narrow alley. The street is about fifty metres long. At the top it is about five metres wide. At the bottom it is 3.2.

This film is about a street, a telephone number, and a definition. Between six thirty-four in the evening and eleven minutes past ten, the police in Itaewon received eleven distress reports about dangerous levels of overcrowding. Officers were deployed to four of them. On the other seven, according to Al Jazeera's reporting at the time, there was no action. Four hours before anything happened, a person standing in that alley used the words crushed to death, and asked for somebody to come and control it.

What the record also holds: 137 police officers were deployed to Itaewon for Halloween, against an estimated hundred thousand people. 81,573 people got off the train at Itaewon station that day, on figures from Seoul Transportation Corporation — 2.6 times as many as the equivalent Saturday a year earlier. And on 23 October 2025, a joint audit by the Office for Government Policy Coordination, the National Police Agency and the Ministry of the Interior and Safety found that the relocation of the presidential office to Yongsan increased the demand for police deployment in the area, which was a key factor behind the lack of crowd control officers in Itaewon. Rallies inside the Yongsan police station's jurisdiction went from 34 in the whole of 2021 to 921 between May and October of 2022.

On 30 September 2024 the Seoul Western District Court sentenced Lee Im-jae, the former chief of the Yongsan police station, at first instance to three years in prison without labour, on charges of professional negligence resulting in death and injury. The same court, the same day, found Park Hee-young, the chief of the Yongsan ward office, not guilty: related law and regulations did not require them to come up with safety measures for events without organizers. On 26 December 2023, the first paragraph of Article 66-11 of the Framework Act on the Management of Disasters and Safety was amended to require a safety management plan for a festival that has no organiser. On the night this film is about, it was not law.

ON THE CASES, AS OF AUGUST 2026
Both appeals are suspended, by the court itself. On 14 July 2025 the Seoul High Court suspended the appeal of the former station chief, and on 28 August a different bench did the same with the district office chief's appeal, until the National Commission for the Investigation of the October 29 Itaewon Disaster and Prevention of Recurrence has deliberated on its findings. Kim Kwang-ho, the former Seoul police chief and the highest-ranking officer indicted over that night, was acquitted at first instance on 17 October 2024. In 2023, all nine judges of the Constitutional Court rejected the impeachment of the Interior and Safety Minister, unanimously. The commission has concluded that it needs until December 2027, and said it would be looking upward — at the chain of command, including the Office of the President.
