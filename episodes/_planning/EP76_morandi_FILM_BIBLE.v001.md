## 0. Read this first: the five ways this film goes wrong

**One. It says somebody knew.** This is the shape every retelling reaches for, and the Genoa court
went out of its way to refuse it. On 16 July 2026 it **excluded the aggravating circumstance of
having foreseen the event for every single defendant** and **acquitted all of them of the
intentional offences "because the fact does not exist"** (MO-119, MO-124). The convictions are for
negligence. If one line, caption, title or thumbnail leaves a viewer believing that a named person
watched the bridge and let it fall, the film has failed — legally, factually and morally. ⛔-01 and
⛔-02 govern every act.

**Two. It becomes a collapse video.** Forty-three people died in the middle of a working morning and
their families are living. There is no falling deck in this film, no vehicle at the broken edge, no
rescue, no body, no memorial with a face (⛔-05, ⛔-06). What we are watching is not a collapse. It
is a sheath, a score, a note, a meeting and a number that was never written down.

**Three. It resolves.** A new bridge was built in two years and it is very beautiful, and it is not
an answer to this film's question. The question is who is responsible for inspecting the thing you
drive across, and a new bridge does not answer it. Use the new viaduct as fact, never as uplift, and
**do not repeat the forty-three-lamps story** — the forty-three light points were in the original
design and the number was reduced (⛔-10, MO-141).

**Four. It blames the man with the clipboard.** The scoring rows are the film's best material and
they are evidence about a system, not about a person. No line attributes a number to a named or
depicted individual; no image invents an inspector's face or thoughts (⛔-07). The point of the
forty is not that a man wrote it. The point is that the company that would have had to close the
road was the company that wrote the rule about when to close it, and the company that scored the
defect, and the company that designed the repair.

**Five. It narrates an English summary and calls it the record.** The load-bearing documents in this
story are Italian and technical, and two of them were read as documents for this film: the ministry
commission's report of 14 September 2018 and the operative part of the Genoa judgment of
16 July 2026. **The 500-page court experts' report was not read**, and every row from it is
SECONDARY and may not carry a beat (⛔-13, ⛔-14, AB-05). Where the record supports a narrower
statement than the story wants, narrate the narrower statement.

## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **The concrete was put around the steel to protect it from corrosion. It is also what made the
> corrosion impossible to see. And every part of the system built to see it instead produced a
> number.**

The stay was designed so that the metal would be preserved (MO-22). Thirty years later, the only
way to know what had happened to that metal was to cut into it, and the ministry's commission says
so in as many words: indirect methods do not permit quantitative assessment; direct investigation is
indispensable (MO-23). What existed instead was a procedure of looking at the outside and writing a
score — a procedure the commission calls **"unsuited to preventing collapses and wholly insufficient
for estimating safety against collapse"**, and which was still, in September 2018, applied to the
whole network (MO-61).

**The one-sentence contradiction** (from the ledger; every act serves it):

> The rule that said close the road was written by the company that would have had to close it; the
> assessment that would have said whether the bridge was safe was owed by law in 2013, was reported
> as done in 2017, had never been made — and all of it was legal until the deck came down.

## 3. MOTIF — five hero objects, and nothing else carries a beat

Every act is anchored to one object. No other object gets a hero close-up.

1. **The cut sheath.** A short length of the stay in section: strands in the middle, grout, a shell
   of precast concrete segments around the outside. It is beautiful and it is opaque. ACT_1, and it
   returns once, silently, in ACT_4.
2. **The external cable.** The 1993 fix at pier 11 — a duct running down the *outside* of the
   concrete, in the open air, where a person can reach it. Put in expressly **"to permit future
   maintenance"** (MO-36). ACT_2.
3. **The score box.** A printed form, ruled, with one empty box waiting for a number. The number is
   composited in Remotion, never generated (⛔-12). ACT_3, and the film's clock.
4. **The empty tab.** A card index or a folder spine with a date on it and nothing behind it: the
   safety assessment owed in March 2013. ACT_4.
5. **The barrier across a road that stops.** Not rubble. A road surface, a line of new steel
   barrier, and beyond it nothing. ACT_5 and the ending.

## 5. REGISTER — the voice

Third person. Past tense. Short declaratives. **No emotion commands** — the words *imagine*,
*shocking*, *unbelievable*, *heartbreaking*, *tragic* and *you won't believe* appear **zero** times.
No rhetorical questions except the two that ACT_1 and ACT_4 exist to answer.

Numbers are spoken plainly and once. Technical terms are defined in the sentence that uses them and
never twice: *stay*, *strand*, *prestressing*, *edge beam*, *infill deck*, *concession*. Italian
words are used only where there is no English equivalent, and each is translated in the same
sentence: *strallo* (stay), *impalcato tampone* (infill deck), *voto* (score).

**Two registers alternate**, and the change is the pacing device:

- **The procedural voice** — flat, specific, present in every act. *"The sheath was built after the
  cables, from precast segments, outside them, and at first free to slide against them."*
- **The consequence voice** — one sentence at a time, used sparingly, never adjacent to itself.
  *"The thing that was supposed to protect the steel is the reason nobody could see the steel."*

**Money is never editorialised.** The line is *"one point three million euros a year became
twenty-three thousand."* It is not followed by an adjective. The commission's own sentence about
maximising profit (MO-45) is delivered **as a quotation, on screen, attributed**, and the narration
does not gloss it.

## 8. THE HOOK — voiced from frame 0, written FIRST

Binding from EP66 onward (spec v3 row 9). Narration audio starts at **0:00 ± 0.5 s**. No outcome is
stated in the first ten seconds. Shape: a time, a place, one act, ending on something nobody in the
scene knows.

> with the concrete gone, the cables showing, and some of the wires broken. Then the form asked for

> the prestressing steel are a seventy. And a seventy means measures up to closing the road."**

**Sixty-seven words.** At the measured 184.0 raw wpm that is 21.8 s of speech, plus two 0.3 s beat
gaps: the hook lands at **0:22.4** and the brand OPENING follows it, clear of the 10–15 s window

**What the hook may not do:** state the collapse, give a death toll, name any person, name the
company as a culprit, use the word *disaster*, or attribute a thought or a motive to whoever wrote

**The forty is the system's, not a man's.** The narration says *"the number written in the box"*,

## 10. PRODUCIBILITY — the staging plan, named before the shot list

**This is the one episode in the batch where the shelf's European bias helps.** For EP73 a European
street is an error; here it is closer to right than anything else on the shelf. But the register is
narrow and two things break it instantly:

1. **American signage, US plates, right-hand-drive traffic.** Italy drives on the right; a
   right-hand-drive vehicle is wrong even in a European street.
2. **Holiday Italy.** Tuscan hills, cypresses, vineyards, Amalfi, gondolas, Roman ruins. Genoa is a
   working Mediterranean port: container cranes, ferries, ochre and grey blocks stacked up steep
   hills, railway yards, warehouses, a concrete-and-gravel river bed, motorway viaducts and tunnels
   running over and through housing.
3. **Italian lettering must be present and unreadable.** A Ligurian street with no writing anywhere
   reads as a set (spec `era_setting`).

| register | plan | ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Port city, hills, dense housing, container cranes | shelf first — Mediterranean port and hillside-city rows; **eyeball a labelled contact sheet before staging** | 45 cuts, ≥28 distinct |
| Motorway viaduct, tunnel portal, deck-level driving | shelf, then commissioned. Reject anything with US shields or UK plates | 40 cuts, ≥24 distinct |
| Concrete: pier, soffit, joint, corroded reinforcement, spalling | commissioned plates plus macro shelf rows; this is the film's texture and it is worth spending on | 40 cuts |
| Inspection: gantry, platform, hi-vis at distance, hand on a rail | commissioned. **No insignia, no readable company mark** | 25 cuts |

## 12. WHAT THIS FILM MUST NOT SAY

The full list is `⛔-01…⛔-14` in the ledger and `forbidden_claims` in the episode spec. The five that
will actually be tempting:

1. **"They knew it would collapse."** Barred outright. The court excluded the foresight circumstance
   for every defendant and acquitted all of them of the intentional offences (MO-119, MO-124). ⛔-02
2. **"Morandi warned them."** Barred in that form. The record establishes that Aspi commissioned a
   report from him in 1981 and that maintenance followed. Narrate that. ⛔-03, AB-04
3. **"The stay snapped."** Not settled. The ministry's commission thought it more likely the trigger
   was an edge beam or a box-girder deck; the court's experts identified corrosion in the pier 9
   south stay. Both, with dates and authors. ⛔-04, AB-03
4. **"Nobody was punished."** Factually wrong and legally careless. The form is: thirty-two people
   were convicted at first instance, twenty-five were acquitted or their charges time-barred, the
   judgment is not final and its reasons had not been written when this film was made.
5. **"The new bridge has forty-three lights, one for each victim."** Barred. Forty-three light
   points were in the original design; the number was reduced. ⛔-10, MO-141

## 12.6 WHERE THE CAPTIONS BREAK

One cue = one breath group. ≤2 lines, ≤42 characters per line, 1.0–6.0 s, ≤17 cps, no orphan
single-word cue, never split a preposition from its noun. Lead each cue 0.60 s (`_smart_split`,
`medium.en`). The phrases that will break badly if left to the splitter:

- "immediate measures, up to closing / the carriageway" — break after *up to*, never after *closing*
- "convicted at first instance / the judgment is not final" — **never** split *at first instance*
- "to preserve the metal part of the stays / from corrosion" — break after *stays*
- "the progressive loss of functionality / of the prestressing cables" — break after *functionality*
- "plausible but not definitive / working hypotheses" — break after *definitive*
