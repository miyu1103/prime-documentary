## A. THE WATER (Acts I and III)

- **CL-01 (H)** Camp Lejeune is a **United States Marine Corps base at Jacksonville, North Carolina**, on the coastal plain. Two of its drinking-water systems were contaminated: **Tarawa Terrace**, which served family housing, and **Hadnot Point**, which served the base's working centre. Src: ATSDR, "About Camp Lejeune." Use: plainly.
- **CL-02 (H)** **Tarawa Terrace's primary contaminant was PCE (tetrachloroethylene).** ATSDR verbatim: *"Water from the Tarawa Terrace water treatment plant was primarily contaminated by PCE."* Src: ATSDR About. Use: plainly.
- **CL-03 (H)** **Hadnot Point's primary contaminant was TCE (trichloroethylene).** ATSDR verbatim: *"Water from the Hadnot Point water treatment plant was contaminated primarily by TCE (trichloroethylene). Other contaminants in the drinking water included PCE and benzene and TCE degradation products."* Src: ATSDR About. Use: plainly. **The degradation products are trans-1,2-DCE and vinyl chloride.**
- **CL-04 (H · the two clean numbers)** **Measured maxima in finished drinking water:** Tarawa Terrace **PCE 215 µg/L**, *"43 times higher than the current U.S. maximum contaminant level (MCL) allowed in drinking water of 5 μg/L"*; Hadnot Point **TCE 1,400 µg/L**, *"280 times higher than the current U.S. maximum contaminant level."* Src: ATSDR, "Summary of the Water Contamination Situation" (verbatim). Use: **these two, and only these two, are the on-screen concentration numbers.** They carry the whole point without arithmetic.
- **CL-05 (H · ⚠ do not mix registers)** ATSDR's **modelled/reconstructed** monthly maxima for Hadnot Point *finished water* are far lower than the well-water figures that circulate: TCE **783 µg/L** (Nov 1983), trans-1,2-DCE **435**, vinyl chloride **67**, PCE **39**, benzene **12** (Apr 1984). Src: ATSDR Chapter A fact sheet (2013). **⚠ CONFLICT ON FILE:** ATSDR's public FAQ page separately lists Hadnot Point benzene *"up to 720 ppb"* and vinyl chloride *"up to 655 ppb."* The two ATSDR products disagree; one is well-water and one is finished-water, and the pages do not reconcile. **Use: NEITHER benzene nor vinyl-chloride concentration goes on screen.** Name the chemicals; quantify only PCE and TCE per CL-04.
- **CL-06 (H · ⚠)** Dr. Michael Gros testified that supply **well 651** at Hadnot Point held *"an astounding 18,900 ppb TCE when finally taken off line 1985."* Src: 2007 hearing. Use: **only if explicitly framed as a WELL, never as what came out of a tap.** Safer: omit.
- **CL-07 (H)** The **statutory** exposure window — used by the 2012 Act, by the VA's presumption rule and by the Camp Lejeune Justice Act — is **1 August 1953 to 31 December 1987**, for service or residence of **not fewer than 30 days**. Src: 38 U.S.C. § 1710(e)(1)(F); § 804(b), Pub. L. 117-168. Use: plainly, as *the period Congress wrote into law*. **Do not call it "34 years of contamination"** without that framing.
- **CL-07a (H)** ATSDR's own **modelled** exceedance periods are narrower and differ by system. Verbatim, ATSDR Director Christopher J. Portier to the VA, **16 Jan 2013**: *"we estimate that the first month any VOC exceeded the current EPA MCL in finished water was August 1953, and at least one VOC exceeded its current MCL in Hadnot Point drinking water from August 1953 through January 1985"*; and *"former Marines and their families who lived in Tarawa Terrace family housing units during the period November 1957 through February 1987 received drinking water contaminated with the dry-cleaning solvent tetrachloroethylene (PCE) at levels above the current EPA maximum contaminant level (MCL) of 5 ppb."* Use: this is the honest scientific spine — the legal window and the modelled window are **not the same thing** and the script says so once.
- **CL-08 (✗ CUT — the brief's flagged figure)** **"~1,000,000 people exposed" is NOT supported.** ATSDR publishes **no total-exposed figure** anywhere on its About, Health Effects, FAQ, Summary, Water Modeling, Tarawa Terrace, Hadnot Point, Public Health Assessment or Health Studies pages. ## C. THE PAPER (Act III — the mid reveal)

- **CL-21 (H · brief corrected · the three documents are DISTINCT)**
  - **(a) OCTOBER 1980.** A US **Army Environmental Hygiene Agency** team from Fort McPherson, Georgia began testing Camp Lejeune water systems for **total trihalomethanes**. The handwritten remark on the **30 October 1980** analysis of Hadnot Point samples, document **CLW 0436**, reads: **"Water is highly contaminated with low molecular weight halogenated hydrocarbons."** Ensminger, sworn, 2007 (original spelling preserved): *"During October of 1980 the United States Army Environmental Hygiene Team from Fort McPhearson, GA. Began testing certain water systems aboard Camp Lejeune for total Trihalomethanes (TTHMs)."* **H.**
  - **(b) DECEMBER 1980.** Document **CLW 0438**, **29 December 1980**: **"Heavy organic interference at CHCL2BR. You need to analyze for chlorinated organics by GC/MS"** — the documented interference with the routine test. **H.**
  - **(c) MARCH 1981 — THE MID REVEAL.** The US Army laboratory's own handwritten remark on the analytical result form, samples **9 March 1981**, Hadnot Point, document **CLW 0443**: **"Water highly contaminated with other chlorinated hydrocarbons (solvents)!"** — quoted in Ensminger's sworn statement, 2007 hearing. **H for wording and document number; M for whether 9 March is the collection or the report date.** Use: **"the samples are dated the ninth of March, 1981"** is the safe form.
  - **⚠ (a) and (c) are two different documents five months apart and are routinely merged in secondary retellings. Do not merge them. Do not attribute either to Grainger.**
- **CL-22 (H)** The mechanism, in a scientist's words — Dr. Michael Gros, sworn, 2007: *"routine water tests typically performed on chlorinated water systems looking for substances called trihalomethanes showed the presence of major contamination with other organic chemicals which required further action. Levels of these contaminants were so high so as to preclude THM testing."* Use: this is the single cleanest explanation of why the alarm existed — **fold it into one sentence in a person's hands, never as a block (R-2).**
- **CL-23 (H · brief corrected)** **Grainger Laboratories, 1982, is a separate private contract lab and a separate event.** 2007 hearing: *"In May 1982, a Grainger laboratory representative phoned Ms. Betz of Camp Lejeune and informed her that they had found high levels of Volatile Organic Chemicals (VOCs) during their analysis of the Hadnot Point and Tarawa Terrace water systems."* The **10 August 1982** letter from **Bruce A. ## E. THE SCIENCE (Act IV)

- **CL-27 (H · the only hard population numbers this film may use)** ATSDR's enrolled study cohorts: **mortality study of Marine and Navy personnel — 154,932 at Camp Lejeune vs 154,969 at Camp Pendleton** (Bove et al., *Environmental Health*, 19 Feb 2014; 8,964 vs 9,365 deaths; follow-up 1979–2008); **mortality study of civilian employees — 4,647 vs 4,690** (13 Aug 2014); **cancer incidence — 154,821 vs 163,484** in the primary analysis subgroup (Bove et al., *EHP*, 24 Oct 2024). ⚠ **Label whichever you use.** The same 2024 study reports a **full cohort of 208,063 vs 225,999**, and ATSDR's January 2024 report was described by AP as "approximately 211,000" vs "approximately 224,000." **Pick one, name it, do not add them.**
- **CL-28 (H · VERBATIM · the causation anchor)** Bove et al., *Environmental Health Perspectives*, **24 October 2024**, conclusion: ***"Increased risks of several cancers were observed among Marines/Navy personnel and civilian workers exposed to contaminated drinking water at Camp Lejeune compared with Camp Pendleton."*** Elevated hazard ratios, verbatim: *"all myeloid cancers (HR = 1.24; 95% CI: 1.03, 1.49), acute myeloid leukemia (HR = 1.38; 95% CI: 1.03, 1.85), myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative syndromes (HR = 1.68; 95% CI: 1.07, 2.62)… and cancers of the esophagus (HR = 1.27; 95% CI: 1.03, 1.56), larynx (HR = 1.21…), soft tissue (HR = 1.21…), and thyroid (HR = 1.22; 95% CI: 1.03, 1.45)."* The authors also state: *"Statistical significance testing was not used."* Use: **"observed," "associated with," "compared with Camp Pendleton" — this is the exact strength of the finding and the script never exceeds it.** ⚠ **There is no "2018 cancer incidence study"** — that is a conflation with ATSDR's 2017 products (CL-29a). - **CL-32 (H)** ***CTS Corp. v. - **CL-33 (H)** What that wall did, in the EDNC's own recital (Order **D.E. 133** at 5–6): pre-Act claimants had filed *"approximately 4,000 claims under the Federal Tort Claims Act… and 17 federal lawsuits."* The MDL court dismissed them on **5 December 2016**, holding *"(1) North Carolina's ten-year statute of repose applied to plaintiffs' claims; (2)… the ten-year limitations period began to run on the date the allegedly contaminated wells were taken out of use; (3) the Feres doctrine barred the claims of service members…; (4) the discretionary function exception… applied."* Affirmed by the Eleventh Circuit in 2019; certiorari denied 2020. **H.** Use: **the reversal-of-fortune setup — the ten-year clock started when the wells were shut, which is to say before most of these people knew anything.**
- **CL-34 (H · statute verbatim)** The **Camp Lejeune Justice Act of 2022** is **§ 804 of the Honoring our PACT Act, Pub. L. 117-168, signed 10 August 2022**. Verbatim (as recited by the court in D.E. 133):
  - **§ 804(b):** *"An individual, including a veteran…, or the legal representative of such an individual, who resided, worked, or was otherwise exposed (including in utero exposure) for not less than 30 days during the period beginning on August 1, 1953, and ending on December 31, 1987, to water at Camp Lejeune, North Carolina, that was supplied by, or on behalf of, the United States may bring an action in the United States District Court for the Eastern District of North Carolina to obtain appropriate relief for harm that was caused by exposure to the water at Camp Lejeune."*
  - **§ 804(c) burden of proof:** *"a party shall produce evidence showing that the relationship between exposure to the water at Camp Lejeune and the harm is (A) sufficient to conclude that a causal relationship exists; or (B) sufficient to conclude that a causal relationship is at least as likely as not."*
  - **§ 804(f) immunity:** *"The United States may not assert any claim to immunity in an action under this section that would otherwise be available under section 2680(a) of title 28, United States Code."* (the discretionary-function exception)
  - **§ 804(g):** *"Punitive damages may not be awarded in any action under this section."*
  - **§ 804(j)(3) the override:** *"Any applicable statute of repose or statute of limitations, other than under paragraph (2), shall not apply to a claim under this section."*
  - **§ 804(j)(2)** set the filing window at two years — **it closed 10 August 2024**. § 804(h) required an administrative claim to the Navy first, with a **six-month** wait. **H.**
  - ⚠ **CORRECTION: there is no statutory bar on jury trial.** The Act's only jury sentence is § 804(d): *"Nothing in this subsection shall impair the right of any party to a trial by jury."* **The bar came from the court — CL-38.**
- **CL-35 (H)** Sovereign immunity: the court held, D.E. 133 at 8, verbatim: *"Subsection 804(b) of the CLJA fits squarely within the Supreme Court's second example. Thus, the United States does not have sovereign immunity for actions under subsection 804(b) of the CLJA."* Use: **Congress removed every barrier it had built.**
