## ★ CONFIRMED FACTS

### The murder & the family (Act I)
- **(H)** Victim: **Christine Morton** (née Kirkpatrick), beaten to death **in her bed** at the family home in **Williamson County, Texas** (the Austin metro area, town of Williamson County near Austin). Bludgeoned — reporting describes a **wooden club / wooden weapon**; the exact weapon was never definitively recovered.
- **(H)** Date: **August 13, 1986** — the morning after the family celebrated **Michael's 32nd birthday (Aug 12, 1986)**.
- **(H)** Michael **left for his job at a grocery store around 6:00 a.m.** (he was a supermarket manager); when he returned he **discovered Christine's body**. Their **three-year-old son, Eric, was in the house.** ⚠
- **(H · ⚠ child witness — restraint)** **Eric (age 3)** later told his grandmother he had seen **"a monster"** — not his father — attack his mother, and that his **father was not home** when it happened. (Both sources.) **Handle Eric with maximum care — a traumatized 3-year-old witness. Do NOT re-create the attack.**
- **(H)** The child's account reached investigators through **Rita Kirkpatrick — Christine's mother / Michael's mother-in-law / Eric's grandmother** — documented in an **interview transcript by Sgt. Don Wood** (lead investigator). This is the "3-year-old's account" AND "Christine's mother's report that the boy said daddy wasn't home" — same underlying evidence, relayed by the grandmother.
- **(H)** **Neighbors reported a man in a green van** repeatedly parked **behind the Morton home** around the time of the murder, the man **walking into the wooded area** behind the house.
- **(M · one-source: Innocence Project)** Prosecution **motive theory: that Michael killed Christine in a rage because she declined sex after his birthday celebration.** SOFT-CITE as "the prosecution's theory." (A note Michael left Christine the morning of the murder figured in Colloff's reporting and "An Unreal Dream," but its **verbatim text is NOT verified this session — do NOT quote it.**)
- **(H)** Michael was **arrested September 25, 1986** and charged with murder. There was **no physical evidence tying him to the crime**; part of the state's case leaned on the **now-debunked claim that time of death can be fixed by stomach contents** (an argument used to place the killing before he left for work).

### The trial & the withheld evidence (Act II)
- **(H)** Lead prosecutor: **Ken Anderson**, the **Williamson County District Attorney** (he later became a state district judge).
- **(H)** **Convicted February 17, 1987**; sentenced to **life in prison.** (Innocence Project adds "plus a $5,000 fine" — soft-cite the fine.)
- **(H)** **Exculpatory evidence withheld from the defense** at trial included:
  - **Eric's account** that a "monster" — not his father — committed the murder (via the Sgt. Wood / Rita Kirkpatrick transcript).
  - The **green-van** sightings.
  - A **bloody bandana** found **near the crime scene** (reporting: roughly **100 yards / about a block away**, near a construction site, recovered by Christine's brother) — later shown to carry **Christine's blood and hair** plus an **unknown male's DNA.**
  - Evidence that **Christine's purse was missing** and her **credit card / checkbook were used fraudulently** after her death (a card reportedly used / recovered in **San Antonio**), and **unidentified fingerprints/footprints.**
- **(H)** Testimony later established Anderson's suppression was deliberate — the record includes his instruction that **"the other side can't have access to those reports"** regarding Sgt. Wood's files. (VERIFIED quote — Innocence Project.)

### 25 years inside & the fight for DNA (Act III)
- **(H)** Morton served **nearly 25 years.**
- **(H)** **2005:** civil attorney **John Raley** (working pro bono) and **Nina Morrison of the Innocence Project** filed to obtain **DNA testing**, including of the **bandana.**
- **(H)** Williamson County District Attorney **John Bradley** (Anderson's successor) **fought the DNA testing for roughly six years.**
- **(H)** **June 2011:** a judge finally **ordered the bandana tested.**

### The payoff cascade (Act IV — stack LAST)
- **(H)** The **bandana DNA** showed **Christine's blood/hair** and the DNA of an **unknown man** — **not Michael Morton** — matched through a database to **Mark Alan Norwood.**
- **(H)** **Michael Morton released October 4, 2011**; **formally exonerated / acquitted by court order December 19, 2011** (Judge **Sid Harle**). He was **the 45th Texas man** whose conviction fell to modern DNA testing.
- **(H)** **Mark Alan Norwood** — a **dishwasher, from Bastrop, Texas, living in the Austin area in the mid-1980s**, a convicted felon — was charged, tried, and **convicted of Christine Morton's murder and sentenced to life on March 27, 2013.**
- **(H · THE GUT-PUNCH)** Norwood's DNA also tied him to the **January 1988 murder of Debra Masters Baker**, **beaten to death in her Austin home in a similar manner** — a killing that happened **while Michael Morton was already in prison for a murder Norwood committed.** **Norwood was convicted of Baker's murder in September 2016.** ⚠ (Debra Baker is a real second victim — treat with restraint.)
- **(H · THE CAPSTONE — prosecutor punished, rare)** A **Court of Inquiry** into Ken Anderson's conduct was held (**February 2013**). On **April 19, 2013**, the presiding judge **ordered Anderson's arrest**, writing that the court **"cannot think of a more intentionally harmful act than a prosecutor's conscious choice to hide mitigating evidence so as to create an uneven playing field."** (VERIFIED verbatim.)
- **(H)** Anderson **resigned as a district judge (September 23, 2013).**
- **(H)** **November 8, 2013:** found in **criminal contempt of court**; **pleaded no contest** in a plea bargain. Sentence: **10 days in county jail (served ~5 with good-behavior credit — released Nov 15, 2013)**, a **$500 fine, 500 hours of community service**, and he **surrendered his law license** (agreed to relinquish his license to practice law; eligible to reapply after five years). Separate **evidence-tampering charges were dropped** in the deal. **He is widely described as having been disbarred; state precisely: he gave up his license to practice law as part of the plea.**
- **(H)** **The Michael Morton Act** — Texas **Senate Bill 1611** — **signed by Gov. Rick Perry on May 16, 2013**, effective **September 1, 2013** — broadened criminal-discovery / open-file evidence-disclosure obligations to require prosecutors to share their files with the defense.
- **(H)** After release Morton **reconnected with his son Eric** (an adult by then, raised believing his father guilty); Morton **remarried (Cynthia "Cindy" Chessman, 2013)** and became a public advocate. Compensation under Texas law reported at roughly **$1.96 million** by 2013 plus an annuity.
