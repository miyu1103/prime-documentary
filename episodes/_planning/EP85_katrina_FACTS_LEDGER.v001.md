# EP85 `katrina` — FACTS LEDGER v001

**The levee and floodwall failures in New Orleans, 29 August 2005, and the litigation that
followed.** Compiled 2026-08-25. The legal spine was read from the opinions themselves.

> ## ⛔ R3 — WHAT THIS FILM MAY AND MAY NOT SAY
>
> 1. **The negligence finding is about MR-GO, and only MR-GO.** Judge Duval found the Corps
>    liable for its **failure to maintain the Mississippi River-Gulf Outlet** channel
>    (KT-201…KT-206). He expressly did **not** find the Corps liable for the LPV levee and
>    floodwall designs — those were already immune, and the plaintiffs did not challenge it
>    (KT-207). **A sentence like "the court found the levees were negligently built" is false.**
> 2. **The finding did not survive.** The Fifth Circuit first AFFIRMED it on 2 March 2012
>    (KT-301) and then, **on rehearing, REVERSED it on 25 September 2012** (KT-302). The film's
>    ending is the reversal, not the trial.
> 3. **The reversal was on immunity, not on the facts.** The court did not find the Corps
>    careful. It held the failure to armour was **shielded by the discretionary function
>    exception** (KT-304). Every sentence about the reversal must carry that distinction.
> 4. **No real-person likeness**, and this episode carries a further rule the owner set: this is
>    a **race-charged** American story and **no face appears on the thumbnail at all**. Inside the
>    film: no identifiable resident, no rooftop rescue subject, no body, no named official.
> 5. **The dead are not a number in the title, a thumbnail, or a beat.**
> 6. Rows marked `⛔` may not be narrated until the flagged source is read.

## A. The channel (S1, S3)

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-101 | **In 1943 Congress requested a report from the Chief of Engineers on ways to make the Port of New Orleans more accessible**; the result, eventually, was the Mississippi River-Gulf Outlet. | S3 (Background) |
| KT-102 | **MR-GO was cut through virgin coastal wetlands at a depth that exposed strata of so-called "fat clay," a form of soil soft enough that it will move if made to bear a load.** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-103 | **"The channel's original designers considered and rejected armoring its banks with foreshore protection, leaving them vulnerable to erosion."** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-104 | **Foreshore protection was authorised for the south shore of Reach 2 in 1967 and costs were charged to the MR-GO project in 1968. From 1968 until 1980, "it is not evident why the protection was not implemented."** In March 1980 the Corps scrapped its original design for further study, and continued studying for two more years. | S3 [VERBATIM of the quoted clause] |
| KT-105 | The 1988 report **concluded the bank-erosion problem threatened to increase dredging costs sixfold.** | S3 |
| KT-106 | **MR-GO eroded to an average of three times its design width.** | S3, quoting S1 at 671 |
| KT-107 | The erosion destroyed the banks that would have helped protect the Reach 2 levee from front-side wave attack, and the widened channel **added more fetch**, allowing a more forceful frontal wave attack. | S3 |

## B. The trial court's finding (S1 — Duval J., 18 November 2009)

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-201 | The claim was brought under the **Federal Tort Claims Act** over the Corps' conduct in the **maintenance and operation** of MR-GO. | S1 at 648 |
| KT-202 | Plaintiffs' theory: **the operation and maintenance of MR-GO caused the Reach 2 levee to be breached catastrophically, flooding Chalmette and the Lower Ninth Ward.** | S1 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-203 | **THE SENTENCE THIS FILM IS BUILT AROUND.** Duval J.: **"It is the Court's opinion that the negligence of the Corps, in this instance by failing to maintain the MRGO properly, was not policy, but insouciance, myopia and shortsightedness."** | S1 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-204 | **"For over forty years, the Corps was aware that the Reach II levee protecting Chalmette and the Lower Ninth Ward was going to be compromised by the continued deterioration of the MRGO."** | S1 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-205 | **"In the event the Corps' monumental negligence here would somehow be regarded as 'policy' then the exception would be an amorphous incomprehensible defense without any discernable contours."** | S1 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-206 | On that reasoning the court found **the discretionary function exception inapplicable** to the failure to maintain MR-GO. | S1 |
| KT-207 | ⚠️ **THE LIMIT OF THE FINDING.** The district court **recognised that MR-GO's design expressly contemplated erosion from wave wash and did not provide for armouring the banks, and held those design features were "shielded by the [discretionary function exception]" — a ruling the plaintiffs did NOT challenge on appeal.** | S3, quoting S1 at 702 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-208 | The trial was a **bellwether**: of seven plaintiffs who went to trial, **three prevailed on all claims and four did not**. A second group had been dismissed before trial on immunity. A third was proceeding to trial. | S2, S3 [VERBATIM figures] |

## C. The appeal — affirmed, then reversed (S2, S3)

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-301 | **2 March 2012 — the Fifth Circuit AFFIRMED.** "We AFFIRM each of the judgments and DENY the petition." | S2 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-302 | **25 September 2012 — on rehearing, the same court REVERSED.** **"We REVERSE each judgment for the plaintiffs, AFFIRM each judgment for the government, and DENY the petition as moot."** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-303 | The discretionary function exception bars suit on any claim **"based upon the exercise or performance or the failure to exercise or perform a discretionary function or duty on the part of a federal agency or an employee of the Government, whether or not the discretion involved be abused."** 28 U.S.C. § 2680(a). | S3 [VERBATIM of the statute] |
| KT-304 | **THE SENTENCE THAT ENDED IT.** **"Whatever the actual reasons for the delay, the Corps's failure to armor timely Reach 2 is shielded by the DFE."** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-305 | The test the court applied: the decision **need not actually have considered any policy implications; it must only be "susceptible to policy analysis."** And under *Gaubert*, **"the very existence"** of a regulation allowing discretion **"creates a strong presumption that a discretionary act authorized by the regulation involves consideration of the same policies which led to the promulgation of the regulations."** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-306 | The court acknowledged the plaintiffs' evidence: **"The Robinson plaintiffs and amici point to record evidence suggesting that the Corps flatly failed to gauge the risks posed by leaving MRGO's banks unarmored."** It answered that much of that evidence went to **negligence in the original design** — which was already immune. | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-307 | A rule the film may state plainly: under the exception, **immunity attaches whether or not the discretion was abused** (KT-303). The reversal is therefore **not a finding that the Corps was careful.** | derived from KT-303, KT-304 |
