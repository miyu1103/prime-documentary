# EP85 `katrina` — FACTS LEDGER v001

**The levee and floodwall failures in New Orleans, 29 August 2005, and the litigation that
followed.** Compiled 2026-08-25. The legal spine was read from the opinions themselves.

> ## ⛔ R3 — WHAT THIS FILM MAY AND MAY NOT SAY
>
> 1. **The negligence finding is about MR-GO, and only MR-GO.** Judge Duval found the Corps
>    liable for its **failure to maintain the Mississippi River-Gulf Outlet** channel
>    (KT-201…KT-206). He expressly did **not** find the Corps liable for the LPV levee and
>    floodwall designs — those were already immune, and the plaintiffs did not challenge it
>    (KT-207). **A sentence like "the court found the levees were negligently built" is false.**
> 2. **The finding did not survive.** The Fifth Circuit first AFFIRMED it on 2 March 2012
>    (KT-301) and then, **on rehearing, REVERSED it on 25 September 2012** (KT-302). The film's
>    ending is the reversal, not the trial.
> 3. **The reversal was on immunity, not on the facts.** The court did not find the Corps
>    careful. It held the failure to armour was **shielded by the discretionary function
>    exception** (KT-304). Every sentence about the reversal must carry that distinction.
> 4. **No real-person likeness**, and this episode carries a further rule the owner set: this is
>    a **race-charged** American story and **no face appears on the thumbnail at all**. Inside the
>    film: no identifiable resident, no rooftop rescue subject, no body, no named official.
> 5. **The dead are not a number in the title, a thumbnail, or a beat.**
> 6. Rows marked `⛔` may not be narrated until the flagged source is read.

## A. The channel (S1, S3)

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-101 | **In 1943 Congress requested a report from the Chief of Engineers on ways to make the Port of New Orleans more accessible**; the result, eventually, was the Mississippi River-Gulf Outlet. | S3 (Background) |
| KT-102 | **MR-GO was cut through virgin coastal wetlands at a depth that exposed strata of so-called "fat clay," a form of soil soft enough that it will move if made to bear a load.** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-103 | **"The channel's original designers considered and rejected armoring its banks with foreshore protection, leaving them vulnerable to erosion."** | S3 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-104 | **Foreshore protection was authorised for the south shore of Reach 2 in 1967 and costs were charged to the MR-GO project in 1968. From 1968 until 1980, "it is not evident why the protection was not implemented."** In March 1980 the Corps scrapped its original design for further study, and continued studying for two more years. | S3 [VERBATIM of the quoted clause] |
| KT-105 | The 1988 report **concluded the bank-erosion problem threatened to increase dredging costs sixfold.** | S3 |
| KT-106 | **MR-GO eroded to an average of three times its design width.** | S3, quoting S1 at 671 |
| KT-107 | The erosion destroyed the banks that would have helped protect the Reach 2 levee from front-side wave attack, and the widened channel **added more fetch**, allowing a more forceful frontal wave attack. | S3 |

## C. The appeal — affirmed, then reversed (S2, S3)

| KT-507 | **The near-miss.** The wall opposite the north breach moved and tilted but held: **"It seems reasonable to assume that the wall… must have been close to failure."** ILIT calls it a failure **"'frozen' in place as a partially developed or incipient failure."** | S4 Vol.V p.V-67; S5 p.8-20 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-508 | **ILIT on all three canals.** **"None of these failures were the result of overtopping; surge levels in all three drainage canals were well below the design levels, and well below the tops of the floodwalls."** | S5 p.xxiv [VERBATIM] |

## F. The test that was run, and then not used (S5) — THE SPINE OF ACT_3

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-601 | **They built a full-scale test on purpose, and it was a good decision.** To design floodwalls on soft ground in a narrow right-of-way, the New Orleans District **"made an admirable decision to construct a test section and perform a full-scale test of this type of design."** | S5 p.8-13 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-602 | **Where and when.** A site in the **Atchafalaya basin, about 80 miles west**, was chosen because **"The swampy foundations soils at this test site were remarkably similar to those at the north end of the 17th Street canal in New Orleans."** (The E-99 test section; the load-test report is Jackson, 1988, US Army Engineer Division, Lower Mississippi Valley.) | S5 pp.8-13, 8-48 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-603 | **WHAT THE TEST SHOWED — in 1977.** **"a gap opened between the sheetpile curtain and the outboard side earthen embankment during loading… and then water penetrated into this gap. This effectively cut the supporting embankment in half, and the water pressures applied against the lower sheetpile sections helped to push the inboard half of the embankment, as well as the I-wall and its supporting sheetpile curtain, towards the inboard (protected) side. This was a failure mechanism that had not traditionally been considered in the local design of floodwall systems in the New Orleans District."** | S5 pp.8-13–8-14 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-604 | **AND THEN IT WAS NOT USED.** **"Unfortunately, despite publication of these important findings in both internal USACE reports as well as in electronic professional journals… and despite the fact that these studies had been undertaken to facilitate the design of the challenging floodwalls along the drainage canals and the IHNC, neither of these lessons were then incorporated in the subsequent design of the floodwalls along the 17th Street, Orleans and London Avenue drainage canals, nor along the IHNC."** | S5 p.8-14 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-605 | As a formal ILIT finding: the mechanism the field test disclosed **"proved to be the actual field failure mechanism at this site,"** and **"was not among the suite of cases/mechanisms analyzed in the original design studies."** | S5 p.8-34 (Finding 9) [VERBATIM] |
| KT-606 | **It was not a surprise to anyone who had read the file.** **"It was, however, not an unexpected mode of failure as it had been clearly evinced in the E-99 full-scale test section experiment… in 1977."** ILIT adds: **"Also, we had read the E-99 full-scale test section reports, and were aware of the likelihood of this mechanism."** | S5 pp.8-19–8-20 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-607 | **The gap, generalised across the system.** **"The foundation-induced breaches had the common element that a gap opened between the levee and floodwall, on the canal side of the wall, as the water rose against the wall."** And: **"Formation of a gap on the canal side of the wall… causes a very significant reduction in the value of the calculated factor of safety."** | S4 Vol.V pp.V-123, V-68 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-608 | **The safety factor.** **"the use of a design Factor of Safety of only 1.3 was also a major problem… this was far too low a value for a system protecting a large urban population."** ILIT traces it back to standards **for levees protecting agricultural land** in the first half of the last century. | S5 p.8-34 (Finding 10) [VERBATIM] |
| KT-609 | **A second ignored test, same section.** Two permeability tests concluded underseepage was not a risk — **"That would prove to be a very serious error, and would result in sheetpiles throughout the system… routinely being far too short to adequately cut off underseepage."** | S5 pp.8-14–8-15 [VERBATIM] |

## G. The design standard (S6, S4)

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| KT-701 | Congress directed the Corps to design for **"the most severe combination of meteorological conditions that are considered 'reasonably characteristic' of the region"** — implemented as the **standard project hurricane (SPH)**. | S6 p.20 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-702 | The SPH criteria came from a **1959** report **"based on historic hurricanes from 1900 to 1956."** | S6 p.20 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-703 | **THE UPDATE THAT WAS NOT APPLIED.** In 1979 NOAA issued revised SPH criteria incorporating later hurricanes **including Betsy, which directly hit New Orleans**. **"It does not appear that the USACE used the updated SPH criteria, however."** | S6 p.20 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-704 | **"The definition 'reasonably characteristic' implies that the SPH is not an extreme hurricane event."** ASCE's own section heading: **"Hurricane Protection System Under-designed."** | S6 p.65 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-705 | The Corps **"Chose SPH meteorological parameters at the low end of the range of 101 to 111 mph"** listed in 1959 as representative maximum winds for a hurricane striking New Orleans. | S6 p.65 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-706 | **"Levee and I-walls were designed to provide protection up to the estimated water elevations for the SPH. They were not designed to withstand overtopping. There was no armoring or uniform use of erosion resistant soils."** | S4 Vol.I p.I-120 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-707 | ASCE: **"It appears that Hurricane Katrina had a probability-based recurrence interval in the range of 50 to 500 years."** | S6 p.63 [VERBATIM] |
| KT-708 | ⚠️ **THE MR-GO SURGE MYTH IS DEAD — and this matters because the LAWSUIT was about MR-GO.** IPET: **"The Mississippi River Gulf Outlet (MRGO) channel, presumed to be a major factor in propagating storm surge into the IHNC, was demonstrated to have little impact on storm water levels for large storms."** The trial court's liability finding rests on MR-GO's **bank erosion and the loss of the wetlands and levee protection** (KT-107), not on MR-GO funnelling surge. **Do not conflate the two.** | S4 Vol.I p.I-3 [VERBATIM] |
