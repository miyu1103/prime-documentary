## A. The flight

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-001 | **12 February 2009, about 22:17 eastern standard time.** Colgan Air, Inc. **Bombardier DHC-8-400**, registration **N200WQ**, operating as **Continental Connection flight 3407** |
| CO-002 | On an instrument approach to **Buffalo-Niagara International Airport** when it crashed into **a residence in Clarence Center, New York, about 5 nautical miles north-east of the airport** |
| CO-003 | **The 2 pilots, 2 flight attendants and 45 passengers aboard were killed. One person on the ground was killed.** The airplane was destroyed by impact forces and a post-crash fire |
| CO-004 | Both crew were based at **Newark Liberty International Airport** |

## B. What the Board concluded

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-201 | **Probable cause, verbatim: "the captain's inappropriate response to the activation of the stick shaker, which led to an aerodynamic stall from which the airplane did not recover."** |
| CO-202 | **Contributing, all four:** (1) the flight crew's **failure to monitor airspeed** in relation to the rising position of the low-speed cue; (2) the flight crew's **failure to adhere to sterile cockpit procedures**; (3) **the captain's failure to effectively manage the flight**; (4) **Colgan Air's inadequate procedures for airspeed selection and management during approaches in icing conditions** |
| CO-203 | The Board concluded **the captain's inappropriate aft control column inputs in response to the stick shaker caused the airplane's wing to stall** |
| CO-204 | **The stick shaker activated with enough airspeed available to correct the situation** — the wings were level and the load factor was 1 G |
| CO-205 | **After the first stick pusher activation the captain applied a 41-pound pull force to the control column**, and the roll angle reached **105° right wing down** |
| CO-206 | Stick forces at autopilot disconnect were **likely less than 2 pounds** and would not account for his control input |

## C. The machine, and what it was telling them

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-301 | The **low-speed cue** is a red and black bar on the airspeed scale. **If indicated airspeed falls to the top of it, the stick shaker activates**, and the airspeed numbers **change from white to red** |
| CO-302 | The **stick shaker** is a stall warning on each control column, aural and tactile. Both columns shake together because they are interconnected |
| CO-303 | The **stick pusher** puts the elevator 2° nose down after an aerodynamic stall has occurred. **It is a tactile instruction to push forward, not pull back.** It can be overpowered by the crew |
| CO-304 | On the ice protection panel, on the captain's side, is a **"REF SPEEDS" switch**, INCR or OFF. Set to INCR it **advances the stall warning so the shaker fires at a lower angle of attack**, restoring the same margin in icing that a clean wing has |
| CO-305 | The CVR recorded the captain stating the **24-hour ice protection check** had been done. The first officer confirmed it **after noticing in the logbooks that a previous crew had not completed the daily check** |

| CO-402 | **14 May 2002** — disapproved for the **commercial single-engine land certificate**, initial, in a Cessna C-177. **Disapproved tasks: takeoffs, landings, go-arounds and performance manoeuvres.** Passed 25 June 2002. At that point he had **296 hours total flight time** |
| CO-403 | **9 April 2004** — disapproved for the **commercial multi-engine land certificate**, initial, in a PA-44. **The entire flight portion.** Passed 29 April 2004 |
| CO-404 | **15 October 2007 — disapproved for the airline transport pilot certificate, initial, while already a Colgan first officer**, in a Saab 340. The disapproved task was **approach and landing with a powerplant failure**. Passed 18 October 2007 |
| CO-405 | **3 October 2007** — an **unsatisfactory grade on his upgrade proficiency check** in the Saab 340. The same task: approach and landing with a powerplant failure. Retrained and passed 15 October 2007 |
| CO-406 | **28 October 2005** — graded **"train to proficiency"** on his initial proficiency check at Colgan as a first officer |
| CO-407 | For scale: the **2008 overall failure rate for an original-issue ATP certificate was 13.1 %**. Failures are not rare, and the Board says so |

## E. The question the airline asked, and the answer it got

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-501 | Colgan's employment application asked: **"have you ever failed any proficiency check, FAA check ride, IOE or line check?"** |
| CO-502 | On his **2005** application the captain answered **yes**, and described only the instrument rating: *"I missed the NDB approach, received additional instruction, then repeated the approach and passed."* |
| CO-503 | **He did not disclose the two other certificate disapprovals** he held at that time (CO-402, CO-403) |
| CO-504 | **At the May 2009 public hearing, Colgan's vice-president of administration stated that if the captain's incomplete answer had previously been known, it would have resulted in his dismissal from the company** |
| CO-505 | The report devotes sections to **Pilot Training Records** and the **Pilot Records Improvement Act** — the machinery by which one employer can see what another employer, or the FAA, already knows |

| id | fact |

| CO-602 | Previously a flight instructor: **Sawyer Aviation, Scottsdale, Arizona** (Aug–Dec 2006) and **Sabena Airline Training Center, Phoenix** (Jan 2007–Jan 2008) |

## G. The company

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-701 | **In 2008 the average salary of a Colgan Q400 captain was $67,000 and of a first officer $24,000** |
| CO-702 | **Colgan did not have locality pay for its pilots. Its management personnel received locality pay** |
| CO-703 | The captain wanted a **crash pad** near Newark but, a colleague said, tried to avoid paying for one by bidding trips with overnights or commutable ends; he **slept in hotels or the Newark crew room** |
| CO-704 | The company handbook current at the time of the accident, dated March 2008, **did not include guidance** on commuting to base on the day of a duty period |
| CO-705 | The Board records that pilots who commute **have a significant responsibility to arrive fit for duty**, and that Colgan **does not require any particular arrangement** for commuting pilots |

## G2. The eighteen seconds, and the word nobody typed

**Added 2026-08-24 on a second pass through S1. These change the film.**

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-1001 | **As the flying pilot, the captain should have been alerted to the rising low-speed cue and should have taken corrective action during the EIGHTEEN SECONDS between the first appearance of the cue and the onset of the stick shaker** |
| CO-1002 | The cues gave **adequate time for the pilots to initiate corrective action, but neither pilot responded to the presence of these cues** |
| CO-1003 | **The airplane was not equipped with autothrottles.** Power was controlled manually by the captain throughout, and both pilots knew this from their training |
| CO-1004 | Colgan's performance system, **AeroData**, gave the crew a **Vref of 118 knots at 21:53** |
| CO-1005 | **A crew could type the keyword "icing" into the remarks box of the AeroData request.** Doing so **increased the Vref by 20 knots for a flaps 15 landing and 15 knots for flaps 35** |
| CO-1006 | A crew could also type **"eice"** — en route ice accumulation — which **added 90 pounds to the calculated landing weight** |
| CO-1007 | **The accident flight crew did not enter the keyword "icing" or the keyword "eice"** |
| CO-1008 | Colgan's own approach speed criteria: **before gear extension, a minimum of 180 knots; before the final approach fix, a minimum of 160 knots; on final approach, Vref +10 / −0 — no airspeed below Vref was allowed** |

## G3. The crew room memo — signed by both of them

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-1101 | **Colgan had a policy prohibiting crew members from using the crew room to sleep overnight** |
| CO-1102 | A **read-and-sign memo of 24 May 2008** from the Newark regional chief pilot stated, verbatim: **"If a Crew Member is based in EWR [then] you are responsible for your own overnight accommodations. Sleeping in Operations or any crew room in EWR is strictly prohibited and will have severe disciplinary consequences, up to and including termination."** |
| CO-1103 | **Company records indicated that the accident captain and the accident first officer acknowledged receipt of this policy information** |

**Hold CO-1102 beside CO-605, CO-701 and CO-702** — a first officer on $15,800, an average
first-officer salary of $24,000, no locality pay for pilots and locality pay for managers, and
a written instruction that finding somewhere to sleep near Newark was her own problem. **State
the four facts. Draw no conclusion; the standard forbids it and the Board drew none.**

## G4. Why the first officer did not speak

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-1201 | In post-accident interviews the first officer was **characterised as assertive**, consistent with the crew resource management training she had received |
| CO-1202 | The Board concluded it is **unlikely her failure to challenge the captain was because of a lack of assertiveness** |
| CO-1203 | **"It is possible that she did not challenge the captain because she did not consider his deviations from standard operating procedures to be remarkable."** |

**CO-1203 is the recognition point of this film.** It is the Board's own sentence and it must be
quoted, not paraphrased.

## H. Fatigue — the Board's careful sentence

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-801 | **The Board concluded the pilots' performance was likely impaired because of fatigue, but that the extent of their impairment, and the degree to which it contributed, could not be conclusively determined** |

**Say it exactly that way.** The temptation is to make fatigue the cause. The Board would not,
and the film may not (`PD_SCREENPLAY_STANDARD` §13: write the claim and the finding apart).

## I. What came afterwards `[SECONDARY UNTIL S3 AND S4 ARE OPENED]`

| id | fact |
|---|---|
| CO-901 | The families of the victims campaigned, and Congress enacted the **Airline Safety and Federal Aviation Administration Extension Act of 2010** |
| CO-902 | It required **all first officers to hold an airline transport pilot certificate** — the "1,500-hour rule" |
| CO-903 | It required **safety management systems** at commercial air carriers |
| CO-904 | **It required the FAA to create a centralised database of FAA and air carrier pilot records, retained for the life of a pilot, which airlines must review when hiring** |

**CO-904 is the payoff of this film and it is currently second-hand. Open S3 and S4 first.**

## J. `forbidden_claims` — the sentences this film may not say

1. **That fatigue caused the crash.** The Board could not conclusively determine it (CO-801)
2. **That the captain lied on his application.** The record says his answer was **incomplete**, and that Colgan said a complete answer would have ended his employment (CO-502, CO-503, CO-504)
3. **That the first officer's pay or her commute caused the accident.** Neither is in the probable cause or the four contributing factors (CO-201, CO-202)
4. **That the 1,500-hour rule would have prevented this crash.** **The captain held an ATP. The rule addresses first officers** (CO-404, CO-902)
5. **That the crew were unqualified.** Both held current certificates and met the requirements in force (CO-601, CO-604)
6. Any characterisation of either pilot's state of mind, feelings, or home life beyond the record
7. Any detail of the deaths, the fire, or the people in the house on the ground
8. That the pilot records database exists **because** of this crash, stated as fact, until S3 is opened
