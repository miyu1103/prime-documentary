## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **A building does not fall in thirteen seconds. It falls for forty years, in writing, in front of everyone.**

Never stated in narration. Carried by the chronology alone: **1981 too narrow → 2018 written down → April 2021 written down again, harder → early June 2021 it begins → 24 June it arrives.**

## 2. THE DRAMATIC PROBLEM, AND WHY ANYONE STAYS 40 MINUTES

The audience knows the ending before the first sentence. That is the asset, not the problem — it is the Titan mechanism, and Titan is the single best-performing video this channel has made (36 min, 1,868 watch-minutes, 0.96 retention at 30 seconds, then flat from 25% to 95%).

So the film is **not** built on "what happened". It is built on one question the audience cannot answer and the people on screen cannot either:

> **How long had it already been happening?**

That question can be asked five times and answered differently each time — 2018, 2021, three weeks, 1981 — and each answer is worse than the last. That is the ladder, and it is what fills forty minutes without padding.

**Dramatic irony runs the entire runtime.** After the cold open, every ordinary scene — a pool deck in the morning, a board meeting about money, a repair contract signed — is watched by an audience who knows. Nothing needs to be underlined. The film's discipline is to let the ordinary stay ordinary.

**The villain is the record, not a person.** No living person's intent is characterised anywhere. Five documents do the work: a 1981 design, an October 2018 report, a set of November 2018 minutes, an April 2021 letter, a June 2026 federal finding.

### ACT I · 1:10–8:00 · **THE BUILDING** **[1,050 w]**
*Register: wonder → domestic → technical calm.*

- **1:10** Surfside, Florida. A twelve-storey condominium on the beach, finished 1981. 136 units. *(CT-03)*
- **1:40** **O1 planted.** What a pool deck actually is: a flat concrete slab with a garage underneath it, and cars parked under the water.
- **2:30** *Relief beat.* Why people bought here. Ordinary, warm, unremarkable. No irony in the writing — the audience supplies it.
- **3:30** **O5 planted.** A column head where it meets the slab. The connection that carries everything, filmed plain.
- **4:20** How a flat-plate structure works, in one paragraph, through objects rather than doctrine. **No block of exposition longer than 20 s** (R-2).
- **5:10** **O2 planted.** Drainage. A drain that sits slightly high. Said once, without weight.
- **6:20** **O3 planted.** Planters, sand, pavers — the things put on top of a slab over forty years.
- **7:10** Salt air. What the sea does to steel inside concrete, said as chemistry and not as menace.
- **⟨HELD⟩ 7:45** The building stood for thirty-seven years without incident.

### ACT II · 8:00–15:00 · **2018, IN WRITING** **[1,250 w]**
*Register: procedural dryness → disquiet → restraint.* **This act is the film's spine.**

- **8:00** October 2018. An engineer, Frank Morabito, walks the garage and the pool deck. *(CT-10)*
- **8:40** *Relief beat.* An ordinary working day: a torch, a hammer, a clipboard.
- **9:30** **L2 opens.** What he found, in his words: failed waterproofing **"causing major structural damage to the concrete structural slab below these areas"**, with **"abundant concrete cracking and spalling"**. *(CT-10 — ✓ verbatim, attributed)*
- **10:30** ⟨60-second pronoun-withheld delay⟩ — the report is described before it is named (R-23).
- **11:00** **L3 opens.** The repairs were priced. **$9.1 million.** *(CT-11)*
- **12:00** **O4 planted.** The report goes to the town. Board member **Mara Chouela** forwards it two days before the November 2018 board meeting. *(CT-12)*
- **⟨HELD⟩ 12:40** **THE ACT'S REVEAL.** At that meeting, Surfside's chief building official **Ross Prieto** told the board the building was **"in very good shape."** It is in the minutes. *(CT-13 — ✓ verbatim, from the record)*
- **13:30** What that sentence did: nothing was forbidden, nothing was ordered.
- **14:20** In 2021, asked about the report, Prieto said he did not remember receiving it and declined to comment on legal advice. *(CT-14)* — **stated flat. No characterisation of his state of mind.**

### ACT III · 15:00–20:00 · **THE MONEY** **[900 w]**
*Register: mundane → exasperated → human.* The gap between a report and a repair.

- **15:00** Three years. A changing board. 136 owners who must each agree to pay.
- **16:20** *Relief beat, genuinely comic.* The arithmetic of a condominium assessment. Allowed to be funny; it is funny.
- **17:30** **L3 fed.** The number has moved. The 2018 estimate was $9.1 million. *(CT-11)*
- **18:40** The assessment passes. **False relief** — on paper, this is the story of a building that fixed itself.
- **⟨HELD⟩ 19:40** — then the letter.
- **20:00** **MID REVEAL (50%).** 9 April 2021. Board president **Jean Wodnicki** writes to residents: the damage **"has gotten significantly worse since the initial inspection"**, it is **"accelerating"**, and it will **"begin to multiply exponentially"**. The assessment is **more than $15 million**. *(CT-15, CT-16 — ✓ verbatim)*
  **L2 closes:** they saw it. They wrote it down. Twice.

### ACT V · 26:00–34:00 · **THE THREE WEEKS** **[1,350 w]**
*Register: cold technical → dread → stillness. No relief.* **PRIMARY REVEAL begins here (65%).**

- **26:00** June 2026. NIST publishes what it found, five years later. *(CT-04)*
- **27:00** **L4 fed.** Two connections between garage columns and the pool deck failed in **early June 2021**. *(CT-05)*
- **27:30** **O5 paid.** The mechanism: **punching shear**. The column head goes up through the slab; the load it was carrying moves to columns that were not built to take it. *(CT-06)* — **carried by the Blender section shot, not by adjectives.**
- **29:00** **L4 closes.** The failure spread **over about three weeks**. *(CT-05)* The film returns to the ordinary days already shown — the same images, now read differently.
- **29:30** **O3 paid.** Contributing factors: understrength design, misplaced reinforcement in the pool deck, added load, long-term corrosion. *(CT-09)*
- **⟨HELD⟩ 30:00** **L1 CLOSES — the film's summit.** Judith Mitrani-Reiser: **"In the case of Champlain Towers South, however, these margins against failure were too narrow from the start."** *(CT-07 — ✓ verbatim)*
  The answer to "how long had it been failing" is: since 1981. Every argument about money was about the wrong question.
- **31:30** Glenn Bell's two factors, quoted whole. *(CT-08 — ✓ verbatim)*
- **33:00** What this does and does not prove. The film says plainly that a finding about structure is not a finding about anyone's intent. **This paragraph is not optional.**

## 9. WHAT IS NOT IN THIS FILM

- No depiction of the collapse, the rubble, the search, or any victim.
- **None of the 98 is named** (`FACTS_LEDGER` §6, pending owner ruling). The named people are the ones who wrote things down: Morabito, Chouela, Prieto, Wodnicki, Mitrani-Reiser, Bell, Hanzman.
- No attribution of motive or state of mind to any living person.
- No "if only" counterfactual. The film does not say the repairs would have saved it; NIST does not say that either.
- ⛔ The "less than half the required strength" figure is quarantined and may not appear.
- No recreation of anyone's final moments; no fabricated bodycam, security-camera or timestamp authenticity.
