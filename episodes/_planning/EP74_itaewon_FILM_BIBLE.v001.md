## 0. Read this first: the four ways this film goes wrong

1. **It shows the crush.** It must not, ever, in any form — see ⛔-02. The instant a body appears,
   this stops being a PD film and becomes disaster footage, and the channel cannot come back from it.
2. **It becomes Korea instead of a crowd.** The audience is 91 % male, 93 % over 55, and American.
   They will not stay for a foreign news story. They will stay for **a street they could stand in**.
   The geometry is the protagonist: fifty metres, five metres wide at the top, **three point two at
   the bottom**, uphill (IT-02, IT-04).
3. **It asserts a verdict the record does not have.** No appellate judgment is verified (AB-02). A
   film that says "he was convicted" full stop, in 2026, is a `factual_support` ship-blocker.
4. **It reads as a foreign-city montage.** Tokyo neon, Chinese lanterns, a daytime plaza of clean
   Western pedestrians — the EU-number-plate-in-Texas error of EP62, and the single easiest register
   failure available here (⛔-14).

## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **A rule written for events with organisers cannot see a crowd that has none.**

Every part of the state worked. Officers took the calls. A district office existed. A police station
existed. Laws on disaster and safety existed. And the court that convicted the police chief acquitted
the district officials in the same judgment, on the same day, because **no law required anyone to
write a safety plan for a party nobody had organised** (IT-34, IT-34a). That is the film.

**The one-sentence contradiction** (ledger, verbatim — every act serves it):

> The state had 137 officers in Itaewon and, two kilometres away at the presidential office it had
> just moved into Yongsan, 921 rallies' worth of demand where there had been 34 the year before; it
> took eleven calls across three hours and thirty-seven minutes and sent officers to four of them;
> and when it was over, the court that convicted the police chief acquitted the district officials —
> because no law required anyone to write a safety plan for a party nobody had organised.

## 2. Three problems this film has, and how each becomes structure

| problem | why it is a problem | what it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone knows the ending in the first minute | No suspense of outcome exists | **Suspense of transmission.** The viewer is made to want to know *what happens to a warning after it is made*. The film follows eleven telephone calls through a state, one act per stage of the journey |
| It is a foreign country in a foreign language | The measured audience does not stay for foreign news | **The alley is the character.** Width, length, gradient and a hotel terrace are things anyone can stand inside. The country is where it happened; the street is what it is about |
| Nobody is finally guilty and the case is still open in 2026 | Viewers want the last five minutes to punish somebody | **The open file is the ending.** In June 2026 the commission extended itself to December 2027 and turned toward the Office of the President (IT-49). The film ends on a live investigation and a plastic barrier, and names the dissatisfaction out loud |

## 3. MOTIF — five hero objects, and nothing else carries a beat

One object anchors each act. No other object gets a hero close-up.

1. **THE WIDTH.** A dimension line drawn across an alley: 5 m at the top, **3.2 m at the bottom**
   (IT-02). Drawn in Remotion over a real plate, never generated as text. ACT_1, and it returns
   once, silently, in ACT_5.
2. **THE CALL LOG.** Eleven ruled rows on a card, timestamped 18:34 → 22:11 (IT-13). Four fill;
   seven stay empty (IT-18). Typography composited in Remotion — **never a generated document**
   (⛔ `fabricated_record`). ACT_2, and the film's clock.
3. **THE SLOPE.** A single drawn line rising from left to right, with **no number on it** (AB-01).
   ACT_3.
4. **THE MAP OF YONGSAN.** Two pins, two kilometres apart: the alley, and the presidential office
   the government moved into that year. The largest image in the film. ACT_4.
5. **THE PLASTIC BARRIER.** The temporary median divider on World Food Street, installed to make
   people walk one way (IT-50). ACT_5 and the ending. The state's final answer, at knee height.

**The turn** is at the top of ACT_5. The viewer has spent twenty-four minutes assembling a chain of
people who could have acted, and expects the law to reach them. It reaches one. And then it explains,
in its own words, that the district officials bore no such duty — because the statute knows events
with organisers, and this was not one (IT-34, IT-34a).

**The recognition** is that the thing which failed was not a person. It was a definition.

## 5. REGISTER — the voice

Third person. Past tense. Short declaratives. **No emotion commands** — "imagine", "shocking",
"unbelievable", "heartbreaking", "you won't believe" appear **zero** times (measured research
finding: emotion commands correlate with loss on this channel).

Numbers are spoken plainly and once. **Every clock time is spoken as a clock time**, because the clock
is the plot: *thirty-four minutes past six*, *eleven minutes past ten*.

Korean names are given **once, in full, with the role attached**, then referred to by role. The
audience will not retain six Korean surnames and does not need to; it needs to retain *the police
station chief*, *the district office chief*, *the city police chief*.

**Two registers alternate**, and the change is the pacing device:

- **The procedural voice** — flat, specific, in every act. "Officers were sent to four of the eleven."
- **The consequence voice** — one sentence, used sparingly, never adjacent to itself. "The width of
  the street did not change between the first call and the last one."

**Attribution is spoken, not implied.** Where a line rests on English-language reporting of a Korean
record, the narration names the outlet — *"the Korea Times reported"*, *"a joint government audit
found"* (⛔-11, AB-07). This is register, not hedging: it is what a serious film sounds like.

## 8. THE HOOK — voiced from frame 0, written FIRST

Binding, v3 row 9. Narration audio starts at **0:00 ± 0.5 s**. No outcome, no toll, no country-level
framing in the first ten seconds. Shape: a time, a place, one person doing one thing, ending on
something the subject does not know.

> **"Thirty-four minutes past six, on a Saturday evening in Seoul, someone pushes their way out of a
> crowded alley, takes out a phone, and calls the police. It is not a crime they are reporting. They
> say people are being forced uphill into a place where there is no room to come back down, and that
> somebody should come and control it. Ten more people are going to make that same call."**

Seventy-one words. At the model's 171.79 wpm that is 24.8 s; **at EP72's measured 191.7 wpm it is
22.2 s** — trim in the edit to land at 0:20 with the last clause intact. **Measure this episode's own
ACT_1 before trusting either number** (§13, spec `notes`).

**What the hook may not do:** give the toll, name the district as the site of a disaster, name any
official, use the words *disaster*, *tragedy* or *stampede*, or identify the caller in any way
(⛔-13). The number **159** does not appear until 14:00 (F39).

## 10. PRODUCIBILITY — the staging plan, named before the shot list

**This is a 2022 story and 93.5 % of the shelf is modern stock. That is this episode's structural
advantage and it is why this topic was chosen over the 1911/1987/1988/1989 candidates.** The trap is
not the period. It is the country.

| trap | rule |
|---|---|
| Tokyo / Shanghai / Hong Kong standing in for Seoul | Blocked in `forbidden_subjects`. Japanese kana and Chinese characters as shopfronts are the EU-plate error. Korean signage or **unreadable** signage only |
| Western daytime pedestrian crowds | Wrong register at any density. This is **night**, **narrow**, **sodium and LED**, **wet-looking** |
| Festival / parade / stadium / concert stock | Blocked. Reads as fun. The one exception is costume **silhouette at distance** |
| Megacity drone skylines | Wrong geometry. A 3.2-metre backstreet is not an establishing aerial |
| Wide flat plazas at correct crowd density | **Density is not the constraint — the walls are.** A clip with the right crowd and no walls is wrong for this film |
| Full COVID masking as the crowd's ordinary state | Out of period: this is the first Halloween after the outdoor mandate lifted |

**Footage review is mandatory** (`footage_review_required: true`). A labelled contact sheet is opened
by a person before any clip enters a cut — and the shelf's labels are known to be broken
(`pd-factory-shelf-mislabeled`: `evidence_bag` returned cartoons). Machine gates cannot see this.

## 11. PEOPLE — required, and none of them real

**24 people plates minimum** (spec), above EP72's 20, because **the crowd is the subject**.

**Owner decision, 2026-08-21: faces are permitted and wanted.** The `[NEG]` block must **not** contain
`human face`, `facial features`, `eye contact` or `headshot` — that instruction erased the people from
earlier episodes. It **must** contain `identifiable person`, `recognisable person`,
`likeness of a real individual`, which satisfies the NEG gate and keeps the crowd human.

Every face must be plainly fictional, and the craft that guarantees it is also the craft that makes
the film good:

- distance, and depth of field that puts the face behind the plane of focus
- motion blur at the shutter speed a real photograph of a moving crowd would have
- partial faces at the frame edge; three-quarter and rear-three-quarter angles
- backlight, rim light, silhouette against signage
- backs of heads, shoulders, hands, coats, breath

**No cut holds one face in the alley.** No plate contains a legible name, badge, unit marking or
readable Korean text. Costumes are colour and shape, never a recognisable character mask that would
carry a third-party mark (`rights_and_licence`).

## 14. HUMAN MUST CONFIRM — eight things no gate measures

1. **No frame anywhere contains a body, a fallen person, or a covered figure.** Watch it. The gates
   cannot see this and it is the one failure the channel cannot recover from.
2. **No face is held in the alley**, and no generated face reads as a specific real person.
3. **Every sign in every frame is Korean or unreadable.** No kana, no Chinese shopfront, no gavel.
4. **Every named person carries their legal status in the same sentence**, with its date and court.
5. **The appellate status was re-verified on the day of the ship**, not on 2026-08-21.
6. **The number spoken is 159**, everywhere, including title, thumbnail and description.
7. **The footage contact sheet was opened by a person** and the shelf's labels were not trusted.
8. **The film was watched once, end to end, at owner standard** before anyone was shown it —
   hook first, captions readable, no paper-slideshow stretch, material not repeated, the ending
   landing on a musical resolution rather than a cut (`feedback_retro_ep37_florence`).
