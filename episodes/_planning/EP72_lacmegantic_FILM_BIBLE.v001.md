## 0. Read this first: the four ways this film goes wrong

**One. It blames the engineer.** This is the default shape of the story and it is the shape the
company's own chairman pushed on the day. Three men stood trial and **a jury acquitted all three**
(LM-35). If a single frame, line, caption, figure card, title or thumbnail leaves a viewer thinking
one man did this, the film has failed — legally, factually and morally. ⛔-01 governs every act.

**Two. It becomes a disaster reel.** Forty-seven people died in the middle of their own town. The
temptation is fire. The film shows the fire as **light on rooftops and smoke against sodium lamps,
from a distance, and never with a person in frame** (⛔-05, ⛔-10). What we are watching is not an
explosion; it is a bearing, a bill, a test, and a shift ending.

**Three. It over-claims the crew rule.** "One man instead of two" is the line every retelling
reaches for. The investigation **could not conclude that a second crew member would have prevented
it** (LM-25, AB-02) and single-person operation is **not among the eighteen causes and contributing
factors** (AB-03). Say what the record says or say nothing.

**Four. It has nowhere to end.** There is no villain sentenced, no reversal, no vindication. The
ending is an absence (AB-01) and it has to be *built* — as the film's argument, not as a shrug.

## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **A system can be arranged so that every step is somebody's job and the whole is nobody's.**

The repair was signed off. The securement followed the rules as the company wrote them. The test was
the test the company specified. The regulator had approved the operation and had not audited the
safety system for eight years. Everything was in order and the town burned. That is the film.

**The one-sentence contradiction** (from the ledger, and every act serves it):

> The regulator had approved the practice, the company had built the schedule, the repair had been
> signed off — and when the town burned, the only people who stood trial were the three employees
> who had carried it out, and a jury acquitted all three.

## 2. Three problems this film has, and how each becomes structure

| problem | why it is a problem | what it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| The audience knows the ending from the first minute | No suspense of outcome is available | **Suspense of mechanism.** The viewer is made to want to know *how a parked train releases itself*. Every act answers one link and opens the next |
| The cause is eighteen things | Eighteen findings is a list, and a list is not a film | The eighteen are never listed. **Five are dramatised as objects** (§3) and the number itself lands once, in ACT_4, as a figure beat |
| Nobody is guilty and that is unsatisfying | Viewers want the last five minutes to punish someone | The last act is built as **"who answered"** — a company that went bankrupt, twenty-five firms that paid, one that refused, three employees who were tried and acquitted. The dissatisfaction is the point and it is named out loud |

## 3. MOTIF — five hero objects, and nothing else carries a beat

Every act is anchored to one object. No other object gets a hero close-up.

1. **The hand-brake wheel.** A wheel a person turns by hand, one car at a time, in the dark. Seven
   were set. The investigation concluded at least seventeen were needed (LM-09, LM-10). ACT_2.
2. **The air-pressure gauge.** A needle falling slowly for ninety minutes while nobody watches it.
   ACT_3, and the film's clock.
3. **The polymer patch.** A grey, slightly glossy material sitting where steel should be — the
   October 2012 repair (LM-21). ACT_1, and it returns once in ACT_4.
4. **The shipping document.** A blank card. Its typography is composited in Remotion, never
   generated (⛔ fabricated_record). It described a cargo less volatile than the cargo was (LM-28).
   ACT_4.
5. **The empty chair in a public room.** Not a courtroom — a plain room with chairs and a table.
   ACT_5 and the ending.

## 5. REGISTER — the voice

Third person. Past tense. Short declaratives. **No emotion commands** — the phrases "imagine",
"shocking", "unbelievable", "heartbreaking" and "you won't believe" appear zero times; the channel's
measured comparators use none and PD's own scripts have used nine to fourteen.

Numbers are spoken plainly and once. Technical terms are defined in the sentence that uses them and
never twice: *hand brake*, *air brake*, *compressor*, *grade*, *securement*. Nothing is explained
that the picture already says.

**Two registers alternate**, and the change is the pacing device:

- **The procedural voice** — flat, specific, present in every act. "The engineer set hand brakes on
  five locomotives and two cars."
- **The consequence voice** — one sentence at a time, used sparingly, never adjacent to itself.
  "The thing holding seventy-two tank cars on that hill was an engine that was on fire."

## 8. THE HOOK — voiced from frame 0, written FIRST

Binding from EP66 onward (spec v3 row 9). Narration audio starts at **0:00 ± 0.5 s**. No outcome is
stated in the first ten seconds. Shape: a time, a place, one person doing one thing, ending on
something the subject does not know.

> **"Ten minutes to midnight, in a village called Nantes, a woman looks out at the railway line
> behind her house and sees flames on the front of a parked locomotive. She calls 911. The
> firefighters who arrive do exactly what they are trained to do, and they do it correctly. What
> nobody standing in that yard knows is that the engine they are about to switch off is the only
> thing holding seventy-two tank cars of crude oil on the hill above a sleeping town."**

Sixty-eight words, ~23 s at 173 wpm — trim in the edit to land at 0:20 with the last clause intact.

**What the hook may not do:** name the town's fate, give a death toll, name any of the three men,
or use the word *disaster*. The word "47" does not appear until 17:30 (F49).

## 10. PRODUCIBILITY — the staging plan, named before the shot list

**The setting is francophone Canada in July, and that inverts EP71's problem.** For a Californian
film the shelf's European bias was fatal; here a north-European-looking street reads closer to
Estrie than a Los Angeles freeway does. But the trap is signage: **any English-only shopfront, any
US highway shield, any EU number plate is wrong.** Signage must be French or unreadable.

| register | plan | ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Freight rail at night — tank cars, couplings, a yard | **Eyeball first.** The shelf's rail subtypes are passenger (`train_platform_night`, `subway_train_motion_blur`) and cannot carry this film. Assume commissioned plates plus i2v | 40 cuts |
| Night road / small town street | `small_town_main_street`, `highway_night_long_exposure`, `empty_road_sunset` | 45 cuts, ≥25 distinct |
| Rain, cloud, storm | `storm_clouds_dramatic`, `stormy_sky_time_lapse` — texture only, never a beat | 20 cuts |
| Fire and its light | **No archive fire.** Light, smoke and reflection only, commissioned | 12 cuts |
| Emergency lights | `police_car_lights_night` — used at distance, no insignia readable | 6 cuts |
| Machinery, gauges, workshop | commissioned plates; the shelf's industrial rows are unverified | 30 cuts |
| Documents, files, rooms | commissioned plates, blank grounds; typography in Remotion | 35 cuts |
| Town aftermath | commissioned; **never a casualty, never a mourner** | 20 cuts |

**Three rules that are not negotiable at assembly.**

1. **Run `check_cross_episode_reuse.py` BEFORE staging, not after.** EP68 pinto and EP69 hyatt have
   already spent this shelf's industrial, engineering and emergency-light registers.
2. **Do not solve thinness by generating more AI plates.** The video budget splits proportionally to
   pool capacity; a bigger AI pool pushes archive cuts off screen.
3. **Eyeball a labelled contact sheet** of the rail, street and emergency registers before the shot
   list is committed. `footage_review_required` is `true`.

## 12. WHAT THIS FILM MUST NOT SAY

The full list is `⛔-01…⛔-10` in the ledger and `forbidden_claims` in the episode spec. The four
that will actually be tempting:

1. **"The engineer didn't set enough brakes."** Never in that form. The form is: seven were applied;
   the investigation concluded at least seventeen were needed, possibly twenty-six; and the test the
   company specified could not have revealed the difference. ⛔-04
2. **"If there had been two men on that train…"** Barred. LM-25, AB-02, ⛔-02
3. **"They lied about what was in the tank cars."** Barred. The papers described a less volatile
   cargo; intent is not established. ⛔-06
4. **"Nobody was punished."** Factually loose and legally careless. The form is: a company went
   bankrupt, twenty-five firms paid into a fund, one refused and then withdrew its objection, three
   employees were tried, and a jury acquitted all three.

## 12.6 WHERE THE CAPTIONS BREAK

One cue = one breath group. ≤2 lines, ≤42 characters per line, 1.0–6.0 s, ≤17 cps, no orphan
single-word cue, never split a preposition from its noun. The three phrases that will break badly if
left to the splitter:

- "hand brakes on five locomotives / and two other cars" — break after *locomotives*, never after *and*
- "a minimum of seventeen / and possibly as many as twenty-six" — break after *seventeen*
- "criminal negligence causing death" — never split

## 14. HUMAN MUST CONFIRM — eight things no gate measures

1. That no frame implies any of the three acquitted men is guilty.
2. That the fire is never shown with a person in frame.
3. That no signage in any cut is English-only, and no number plate is EU or US.
4. That the rail material reads as North American freight, not European passenger.
5. That the "17–26" card never resolves to a single number.
6. That the hook's last clause survives the trim to 0:20.
7. That the ending reads as an argument, not as a shrug.
8. That the title, the thumbnail text and the description each pass ⛔-01 on their own, without the
   film's context to soften them.
