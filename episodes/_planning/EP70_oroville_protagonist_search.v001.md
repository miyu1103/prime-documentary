# EP70 Oroville — protagonist search

**Question asked:** can a NAMED, DOCUMENTED ordinary person or family be placed at the centre of the
12 February 2017 Oroville evacuation, from primary or near-primary sources?

**Researched:** 2026-08-12. Every source below was retrieved and read by this agent. Sources that
could not be retrieved are listed in section E and are not cited as evidence anywhere else.

---

## A. VERDICT

**PARTIAL.** Named ordinary people are documented at the centre of the evacuation and its aftermath,
but no single individual has a continuous documented arc; the film must be built on one named family
for the order and one named person for the aftermath, and it is a 30-minute film, not a 45-minute one.

to contain.

1. **CourtListener id 9430451 is a CLUSTER id, not an opinion id.** The opinion id for
   `Oroville Dam Cases`, No. C093600, is **9882308**.
   `https://www.courtlistener.com/api/rest/v4/opinions/9882308/` (retrieved with the repo's

   "evacuat", "resident" and any individual plaintiff name appear **zero times**. The plaintiff is

   > "The Butte County District Attorney brought this action under Fish and Game Code section 5650.1

## C. The contradiction, and the opening beat

The strongest fact found in this search is not in the news coverage. It is in the litigation, and it
is the thing that makes this a *system* story rather than a *concrete* story.

The evacuees sued for something almost embarrassingly small — the out-of-pocket cost of two days.
The State's defence was that the mandatory evacuation had **not actually been mandatory**:

> "The state's Department of Water Resources argued, in filings in the lower court, that the Bechtel
> plaintiffs couldn't constitute a class because it was impossible to determine exactly why different
> people had evacuated. While some counties had issued 'mandatory' evacuation orders, other
> jurisdictions only 'strongly recommended' evacuation. Even a 'mandatory' evacuation wasn't actually
> mandatory, they argued, since there is no penalty for ignoring the order, and no one comes and
> forcibly removes you from your home."

And the trial court agreed the order was too vague to define who was in the class:

> "it would be difficult (and in some cases impossible) for many of the putative class members to
> determine whether they would be eligible to join in the class action. Even the 'mandatory' order is
> not sufficiently detailed to provide enough information to determine if a person actually
> evacuated…"

The Third District affirmed on **15 March 2022**. The Bechtel plaintiffs had sought only

> "to recover out of pocket expenses incurred … during the two-day mandatory evacuation period and
> the value of the loss of use of their properties for the two-day mandatory evacuation period."

**Source (retrieved in full):** Courthouse News Service, "Oroville Dam evacuees can't sue as a class,
California appeals court rules", Hillel Aron, 15 Mar 2022.
`https://courthousenews.com/oroville-dam-evacuees-cant-sue-as-a-class-california-appeals-court-rules/`
(Panel: Renner, Duarte, Mauro. Two plaintiff groups: the **Bechtel** plaintiffs — all 188,000
evacuees; the **Giordano** plaintiffs — diminished property values. Both denied class status in a
90-page trial-court ruling; only Bechtel appealed.)

### The one-sentence contradiction

> A police officer knocked on their door and told them to get out; when they later asked the state to
> pay back what those two days had cost them, the state's answer was that the order had never
> actually been binding — and the courts agreed they could not even sue as a group.

That is a contradiction that transfers. Most viewers do not live below a dam. A very large number of
them have been told to leave — for a fire, a flood, a chemical spill, a hurricane — by a government
whose order turns out, when money is on the table, to have been a suggestion.

### The opening beat

Cold open on the order itself, at a Yuba City petrol station, some time after 4 p.m. on Sunday
12 February 2017: a couple three weeks into a new apartment, two boys and a dog in the car, whatever
fitted in the boot, the rest stacked as high as they could reach in a downstairs flat they expect to
find under water. "The police came and told us to evacuate." Then, over the traffic: nobody had told
them they lived below an unarmored hillside, and somebody had written that down in 2005.

Not the 2005 filing. Not the water. Not the sheriff's conference room. The person, then the abnormal
event, then why, then the system — in that order.

## D. Can the reframe reach personal_threat ≥ 16 and story_arc ≥ 9?

**personal_threat: yes, realistically 16–17. Not 18, not 20.**
The transferable threat is no longer "a dam might fail near you". It is "a government can order you
out of your home and then argue the order was never binding." That is portable to every evacuation
any viewer has lived through, and it is the same shape as the channel's proven winners: an ordinary
person contacts a system on an ordinary day and discovers the system has no door. What holds it below
18 is honest: nobody died, nobody lost a house, and the loss most viewers will register is two nights
and a rejected claim.

**story_arc: 8–9, and 9 only under one condition.**
The condition is that the film commits to **one named family for the order (Levias) and one named
person for the aftermath (Parks, or Morton)** and states plainly on screen that they are different
people. If it instead narrates "residents" and cuts between six people, it stays at 7 and the rescore
was wasted. A composite is weaker than a single life followed end to end — and no single life is
documented end to end here. That ceiling is real and it is the honest answer to the question asked.

**Expected effect on the total: roughly +5 to +7, i.e. 82–84.** That clears IMPROVE and lands in the
v002 RESERVE band. It does not reach the v002 PRODUCTION threshold of 90 on this record, and I would
not claim otherwise to get the topic built.

**Runtime: this is a 30-minute film. It is not a 45-minute one.**
The primary-source spine is strong and finite: the 17 Oct 2005 FERC motion → 12 Feb 2017 order →
the IFT report of 5 Jan 2018 → the state rejecting the claims in autumn 2017 → class status denied
15 Mar 2022 → DWR held not a "person" 5 Oct 2023. Six documented moves. The human record on top of it
is four or five fragments, none longer than a few hundred words. Forty-five minutes of that is
padding, and padding is banned. **Recommend the owner be told the truth: 30 minutes, tight, or pick
another topic.**

## 2.4 The contradiction, upgraded — now sourced to the court, not to a reporter's paraphrase

Section 1 §C built the contradiction on Courthouse News' summary. The primary document is sharper,
and the reason the case died is not the one the news reporting implies. It was not that the order was
"not really mandatory". It was that **there was no defined mandatory evacuation zone at all**:

> "In fact, the evidence and briefing submitted to the trial court demonstrated there was no defined
> mandatory evacuation zone and no defined group to which a mandatory evacuation order was issued."
> — C090941, p. 9

> "they did not describe a specific area but rather such vague areas as: 'low levels of Oroville and
> areas downstream,' the portion of Yuba County that is 'on the valley floor,' and 'other communities
> along [the] Feather River.'" — C090941, p. 9

> "This lack of ascertainability would also prevent individual class members from self-identifying as
> meeting the class definition." — C090941, p. 10

And the trial court, quoted in the opinion:

> "There is simply no means by which to readily determine whether a residence or person falls within
> a 'mandatory' evacuation zone, whether the resident actually evacuated, and which order (if any)
> caused that person to evacuate." — C090941, p. 3

> "because there simply is no typical claim among this group, the Court cannot say that the proposed
> Plaintiffs have claims that are typical of the class members." — C090941, p. 3

DWR's retained expert, Dr. Cova, "an expert on human population evacuations due to natural disasters":

> "the decision of whether to evacuate in response to an environmental threat is left to each
> household or individual." … "Dr. Cova stated there is no known method to enumerate who evacuated
> and who did not in a given area and time frame in response to a broadcasted evacuation order."
> — C090941, p. 7

And the closing line of the disposition, which is the film's last beat:

> "The judgment is affirmed. Respondent Department of Water Resources shall recover its costs on
> appeal." — C090941, p. 12

The people who were ordered out of their homes were ordered to pay the state's costs of proving they
could not be identified as having been ordered out of their homes.

### The one-sentence contradiction, rewritten

> A sheriff's car came down the street and told them to leave; when they tried to make the state pay
> for the two days it cost them, the state's answer — and the court's — was that no one could
> determine who had been ordered to go, because the order had never named anywhere in particular.

That is a *hidden system* premise, not a *disaster* premise. It is the strongest form this topic has
taken at any point in the process.

## 2.7 THE NUMBER — does personal_threat reach 18 and story_arc reach 9?

### story_arc: YES, 9.

The arc is now person-first and continuous end to end, and it is carried by one named woman:

1. Denise Johnson walks into a supermarket in Oroville. *(sworn declaration)*
2. Strangers start shouting; the shop announces it is closing and tells her to go home and leave. *(sworn)*
3. 4:10 p.m., then 4:34 p.m.: failure of the spillway anticipated within 60 minutes. *(DWR's evidence, verbatim)*
4. Why nobody told her: the 17 October 2005 FERC motion; an unarmored hillside. *(CNN / Stork, §B8)*
5. She joins 188,000 people asking the state for the cost of two days. *(her own complaint)*
6. The state's expert testifies no one can determine who evacuated; the court finds there was no
   defined mandatory zone; the class dies; DWR recovers its costs. *(C090941)*
7. And in 2023, the agency is held not to be a "person" who can be fined at all. *(C093600)*

Seven beats, one face, no invention, every step on a document. **9.** I would not claim 10 — the
record of her life ends where the litigation ends, and we never learn what the two days actually cost
her, which a 10 would require.

### personal_threat: NO. 16, at best 17.

I am not going to stretch this, so here is the reasoning plainly.

**What lifts it well above the scored 12:** the threat is no longer "you might live below a dam." It
is "a government can order you out of your home and then be unable to say whom it ordered." Every
viewer who has been evacuated for a fire, a flood, a hurricane or a spill is inside that sentence,
and that is a far larger group than people living below dams. The mechanism is portable, which is
what this axis actually rewards.

**What holds it under 18, honestly:**
- The realised loss to the protagonist is two nights and out-of-pocket expenses. The plaintiffs
  themselves pleaded only "out of pocket expenses … during the two-day mandatory evacuation period."
- Nobody was hurt. Everyone went home in two days. Nothing was permanently taken.
- An 18+ on this axis belongs to the losses the channel's winners are built on — the seized savings,
  the eleven years in a cell, the house sold at auction. Two nights in a car is not that, and
  pretending otherwise is the padding the owner banned.
- To reach 18 I would have to lean on the flood that did not happen. That is a hypothetical, and
  scoring a real film on a counterfactual is exactly the move that produces the "AI-smelling"
  documentary the owner keeps rejecting.

**16, or 17 if the reframe lands perfectly in the writing. Not 18.**

### Consequence, stated plainly

Against the rule given — *if yes to both, make it at 30 minutes; if no, reserve band and the
45-minute slot goes elsewhere* — **the answer is no, and EP70 goes to the reserve band.**

My recommendation, for whatever it is worth alongside that: the material found today is genuinely
good, and this is now a strong **30-minute** film with a sworn ordinary protagonist and a real
hidden-system contradiction. It is not a 45-minute film and it never was. If a 30-minute slot opens,
this is worth building. The 45-minute slot should go to a subject where somebody lost something they
could not get back.
