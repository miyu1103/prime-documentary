### EP57 — SANDRA HEMME · "Every Answer Was Different" · 30 min · ★5/5 · R2

**Angle.** The confession engine at its most extreme point, with a twist no other candidate in the feeder can match: **the man whose own evidence should have ended the case was a police officer, and he was the one they should have been looking at.** A young woman is questioned inside a psychiatric hospital while sedated on antipsychotic medication, and gives a statement that changes every time she gives it. That statement costs her forty-three years — the longest known wrongful imprisonment of a woman in American history. When a judge finally finds clear and convincing evidence of actual innocence, **the state's Attorney General fights the release order anyway, and she walks out only after the judge threatens to hold his office in contempt.** And then, because Missouri's compensation statute pays only DNA exonerees, the state owes her nothing at all. That last fact is the film's present tense, and it is why this case beats every "freed after N years" story on the bench.

**One-line hook (measured grammar — present-tense, stakes gap, numbers on both ends; no colon, no em-dash, no second person, no ALLCAPS word, 6–10 words).**
> **"She Was Sedated When She Confessed. It Cost Her 43 Years."**
> Alternates for the packaging pass: *"A Judge Ruled Her Innocent. The Attorney General Fought to Keep Her In."* · *"The Man They Never Investigated Was Wearing a Badge."* · *"Missouri Held Her 43 Years. It Owes Her Nothing."*
> **Banned for this episode:** "Freed after 43 years", "Exonerated after 43 years", or any resolved-relief construction (R-38). **The 43 is a stake, never the resolution.**

**Story engines fired (CTR_GROWTH_REFERENCE Part F).**
- **① confession / interrogation drama — 5.36×, the highest-lift engine in the corpus, and this is the purest version available anywhere in the feeder.** Not merely a long interrogation: a *chemically compromised* one, inside a psychiatric ward, producing mutually incompatible statements. If the corpus's top engine has a ceiling case, this is it.
- **② vivid named villain — 2.79×.** A police officer, on the record, with contemporaneous physical evidence tying him to the crime that was never followed — **and he is deceased, which makes him nameable against a public judicial finding.** Plus a second, present-tense antagonist: a sitting Attorney General resisting a court's innocence order in the 2020s.
- **④ wronged innocent — 5.3×**, at record scale, and statable as fact because a court found it.

### EP58 — CAMP LEJEUNE · "The Water on the Base" (Jerry Ensminger) · 30 min · ★5/5 · R2

**Angle.** A Marine Corps drill instructor buried his nine-year-old daughter and was told it was nobody's fault. Years later he is in his kitchen with the evening news on when a reporter says the words that make him put the plate down: the drinking water at the base where his wife was pregnant was contaminated. **The film is one man's decades-long document hunt against the institution he gave his life to** — and the institution's own paperwork is the murder weapon. This is not a mass-tort explainer and must never become one. It is a betrayal story with a filing cabinet at the centre of it, and it ends in the present tense, with the government still litigating against people who are dying.

**The angle nobody else has taken (verified this pass).** Existing coverage stops at the harm and the 2012 law. **Nothing covers the 2022–2026 litigation collapse** — the part where Congress finally passed a statute to compensate these people and the government then settled **under 1% of claims** while claimants died. That is the film. The single most devastating number in the entire feeder sits here: of the first 25 bellwether cases, three settled — **for $10,000, $24,000, and $405.** A documentary can be built around that last figure alone.

**One-line hook.**
> **"He Buried His Daughter at Nine. Then He Found the Base's Own Water Reports."**
> Alternates: *"The Corps Tested Its Own Water. It Kept the Wells Running."* · *"Congress Passed a Law to Pay Them. One Marine's Family Got $405."*
> **The stakes gap is the topic's superpower:** a dry cleaner's 250-gallon tank against a million people and 34 years. Numbers on both ends, exactly per R-35.

**Story engines fired.**
- **③ relatable family victim — 1.64×, 22.4% of the top decile.** A named child and a named father, each with one unrepeatable concrete detail (R-22). **This is the engine EP53–56 barely used.**
- **② villain — 2.79× — institutional, made vivid the only legitimate way: by record inventory.** The base's own laboratory reports. The federal health study that was withdrawn. The letters that went unanswered. Never adjectives; always documents.
- **④ wronged — 5.3× — at mass scale**, delivered through exactly one family so it stays a story and not a statistic.
- **① is deliberately absent.** EP57 and EP59 both fire the interrogation/testimony engine; three in a row would be the same film three times. See the engine-monotony guard in §3.

**Act / reveal-ladder sketch.**

- **Act III — Evidence.** The early-1980s reports and what they said about the water; the wells that stayed open; the length of the exposure window. **MID REVEAL ~50%: they measured it, wrote it down, and did not stop.** The verbatim card is **William C. One 20–40 s reset beat at 55–70%. **Second protagonist enters here: Mike Partain**, born in the base hospital, male breast cancer at 39, who personally assembled the male-breast-cancer cluster from 19 to 125+ men — the film's second document-hunter and its register change.

### EP59 — TOFOREST JOHNSON · "The Only Witness Was Paid" · 30 min · ★5/5 on strength · **R3 — CONDITIONAL ON AN OWNER GUARDRAIL EXCEPTION**

**Angle.** A sheriff's deputy is shot dead in a hotel parking lot in 1995. The case that put a man on Alabama's death row rests almost entirely on one person who says she overheard a voice on a jail phone line. **The jury was never told she collected a $5,000 reward.** Across four trials the State advanced as many as five different accounts of who actually pulled the trigger. And now the extraordinary part, which is the whole film: **the District Attorney of the county that convicted him has gone into court and said the conviction has no integrity and that justice demands a new trial** — joined by a former Alabama Attorney General and a former United States Attorney. **The judge has not ruled. It has been pending since March 2025. He is still on death row.** Nothing about this story is in the past tense.
