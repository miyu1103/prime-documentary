## 0. Read this before anything else: the four ways this film goes wrong

**One. It becomes a film about a dam.** It is not. The dam is the weather. The film is about an
order. The premise's own thumbnail reasoning retires the single most striking image in the whole
topic — the eroding hillside beside the intact concrete spillway — on exactly this ground: *it sells
the dam, and this film is not about the dam.* Fourteen of the fifteen clearable minutes of verified
footage are the dam, which means the temptation is not theoretical; it is sitting in the rights file
in 1080p, free, and it will pull the film toward itself every time somebody is short of a shot. The
dam is a rationed cutaway. **It never opens an act and it is never the subject of a beat.**

**Two. It says a disaster happened.** No dam failed. No town flooded. Nobody died and nobody was
hurt. Everyone was home inside two days. Every disaster grammar the archive offers — inundated
streets, rescue boats, rubble, sirens over ruins — is a lie about this story, and one of them is
sitting in the rights file already: several DVIDS captions on California National Guard imagery read
*"in the aftermath of a dam failure near Oroville, California."* There was no dam failure. That
caption phrase is barred in `forbidden_claims` and thirteen inundation words are barred in
`forbidden_subjects` so the build fails on a filename rather than on a viewing.

**Three. It gets the reason the case failed wrong.** Every news account says the plaintiffs lost
because a "mandatory" evacuation order turned out not to be really mandatory — no penalty, nobody
comes to drag you out. That is *not* what the appellate court said, it is weaker than what the court
said, and it is retired. What the court said is:

> "In fact, the evidence and briefing submitted to the trial court demonstrated there was no defined
> mandatory evacuation zone and no defined group to which a mandatory evacuation order was issued."
> — C090941, p. 9

Not *the order was soft*. **There was no zone.** That distinction is the film.

**Four. It sneers at six people.** Francis Bechtel, Jacob Klein, Chantel Ramirez, Denise Johnson,
Marie Giordano and Carol A. Gissell are private individuals who sued and lost. They were accused of
nothing. They asked for the out-of-pocket cost of two days and the loss of use of their homes for
those two days — that is the entire claim, pleaded in their own words. A single line that lets a
viewer think they overreached breaks HC-2 and breaks the film, because the film's whole argument is
that the smallness of the ask is what makes the answer shocking.

## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **An order broad enough to move a city is too broad to prove afterwards — and the person it moved
> is the one who pays for the discovery.**

Stated as the channel's own shape:

```
an ordinary renter          Denise Johnson, walking into a supermarket
  → an ordinary day         Sunday afternoon, 12 February 2017
    → contact with a system an emergency evacuation order
      → an impossible outcome the courts cannot determine that she was ordered to leave
        → why?             because the order never named anywhere
          → the anatomy    what a class needs, and what "the valley floor" is not
            → the outcome  judgment affirmed; DWR recovers its costs on appeal
              → and this concerns you   every evacuation order you will ever be given has this shape
```

**The Hidden Rule, spoken once, at the very end, and never before:**

> An emergency order is written to be obeyed, not to be proved. Those are two different documents,
> and you only ever get the first one.

## 2. Three problems this film has, and how each becomes structure

### Problem 1 — the stakes are two nights, and the record will not let us inflate them

The realised loss to the protagonist is two nights out of a rented house and out-of-pocket expenses.
Nobody died. Everyone went home. `human_stakes` is scored 10 of 15 and the premise says in terms:
this is the axis the film must not inflate, and reaching for the flood that did not happen is a
counterfactual and is exactly the padding the owner banned.

**So the smallness is the weapon, not the weakness.** The film states the size of the claim early,
plainly, almost apologetically — *two days; the cost of two days* — and then spends twelve minutes
on the machinery that was brought against it: a retained expert on human population evacuations, a
90-page ruling, an appeal, and a costs order. The gap between what was asked for and what was
mobilised to refuse it is the emotion. **Never say the loss was large. Show what was spent saying
no.**

The one place the record does authorise a large number is Nicoli Nicholas, and he is deliberately
*not* the spine: at least $150,000, and 81 calves dead by 27 January 2018 of a virus his cows caught
on the pastures they were moved to. He is the mid-point escalation and the proof that "everyone went
home" is not the same as "nothing was lost". He is one beat. He is never the protagonist, because a
rancher sits one step outside the channel's ordinary register and because the arc belongs to Johnson.

### Problem 2 — the reveal is a civil-procedure ruling

`wtf_factor` is scored 13 of 15 and short of 15 for exactly this reason: the detonation needs about
forty seconds of setup. A viewer must understand what class certification is, and must understand it
without being taught.

**So the film never explains class certification as a concept.** It asks a question the viewer
already knows the answer to, and then breaks it:

> A hundred and eighty-eight thousand people were told to leave. Four of them went to court and asked
> the state to pay back what those two days had cost. The state's first move was not to argue about
> the money. It was to ask a question nobody in the room had expected: *which people?*

That is the mechanism, delivered as an event rather than as a definition. The word "ascertainability"
appears exactly once in the film, quoted from the court, and is immediately translated.

### Problem 3 — Answer Reversal, and it is real rather than staged

The v002 editorial direction makes Answer Reversal a script gate: let the viewer form a wrong answer,
take it away, offer a second, take that away, then land the real one. This topic supplies a genuine
three-step reversal and the film is built on it. **This is ACT_4's whole spine.**

| step | the answer the viewer forms | what takes it away |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *They lost because you can't sue the government.* | They could. The case was heard. The State's own expert testified. It went to the Court of Appeal. |
| 2 | *They lost because "mandatory" wasn't really mandatory — there's no penalty for staying.* | That was argued, and it is what the newspapers reported, and **it is not what the court held.** |
| 3 | — | **There was no defined mandatory evacuation zone at all, and no defined group to which any mandatory order had been issued.** The orders named "the low levels of Oroville and areas downstream", "the valley floor", "other communities along the Feather River". A judge could not resolve any of that into a place. |

Step 2 is the one that matters, because it is the version the viewer will have met if they have met
this story at all. The film says out loud that it is the reported version, and then takes it away
with the court's own sentence.

## 3. MOTIF — five hero objects, and nothing else carries a beat

Everything this film is about is either a place with nobody in it or a piece of language. There is no
machine to take apart, no rod, no ledger. So the motif set is deliberately austere.

1. **THE SUPERMARKET DOORWAY.** Interior, looking out through sliding glass into an ordinary Sunday
   car park. It opens the film, it returns at the TURN, and it closes the film. It is the only
   recurring image and it earns the repetition — the third and last time, it is empty and the lights
   are off.
2. **THE OPEN LINE.** A boundary drawn on black that starts, wanders, and never closes. It is the
   film's diagram of the court's finding and it is the C thumbnail. It appears three times: first as
   a confident stroke, then hesitating, then abandoned mid-air. **It is PD's own drawing and is
   labelled as such. It never purports to reproduce a Butte County map.**
3. **THE FOUR TIMESTAMPS.** 4:10, 4:34, 4:59, 6:03 — set as typography on the film's document
   ground, accumulating one at a time down the frame across ACT_1 and standing complete for the first
   time when the fourth lands. A fifth line sits *above* them all and is added last: **3:00 p.m.,
   Francis Bechtel's television.**
4. **THE VALLEY FLOOR.** A flat inland horizon at dusk — orchard rows, a two-lane road, a levee bank,
   irrigation. Never coastal, never a canyon, never a city. It is what "the valley floor" means when
   a court is asked to find its edge and cannot.
5. **THE HEADLAMPS.** Cars leaving, always from behind and always at a distance, always as light
   rather than as vehicles. The film never gets close enough to a car to be inside anyone's panic.
   Panic is not the register (see §5).

## 5. REGISTER — the voice

Declarative. Past tense. No adjective the record does not carry. **Nothing tells the audience what to
feel** — `check_script_craft` fails on emotion commands and the measured basis is that winner material
contains zero across four hours.

Six rules bind every page.

1. **Attribution is spoken, not implied.** "Her account, given later under oath." "A rancher named
   Nicoli Nicholas pleaded." "Ron Stork told CNN." "The court wrote." The film never speaks a
   secondary source in its own voice.
2. **The counties and the State are never confused.** Butte, Yuba and Sutter Counties issued the
   orders. The State of California, through the Department of Water Resources, is the defendant. The
   film's argument dies the moment those two are blurred, and the premise's own broad title blurs
   them — see §14.
3. **"Ordered to leave", never "evacuated"**, when the number 188,000 is in the sentence. DWR's own
   expert testified there is no known method to enumerate who evacuated. The film may not do what the
   expert said cannot be done.
4. **Sixty minutes is quoted, never paraphrased.** The broadcast anticipated *failure* within sixty
   minutes. It did not give anybody sixty minutes to leave. Read the sentence; let the viewer do the
   arithmetic.
5. **No panic vocabulary in the film's own voice.** Genoa Widener's "people were running in the
   streets" and the thirty-foot wall of water are hers, attributed, dated, and framed as *what people
   were told*. The narrator stays level. The archive stays wide.
6. **No spoken call to action.** `check_script_craft.SPOKEN_CTA` is a hard gate wired into the
   acceptance run. The ask lives on the endcard, in the description and in the pinned comment.

**Second person by 5:30** (`verify_script_structure` rule 6). It arrives in ACT_1 at about 4:10, once,
and it is not a rhetorical flourish — it is the transfer: *If you have ever been told to leave
somewhere, you have been inside a sentence like this one.*

## 9. WHAT THE IMAGES MUST CARRY — the motion budget

**Row 8 of the production spec, and the standing owner directive: no static image, ever; no held
frame over 2 s; no naked hard cut; mean shot ≤ ~6 s.** This film's declared `target_cut_sec` is
**3.8**, which is faster than the builder default of 4.6 and is what makes 114 stills fit inside the
32% still-share ceiling.

**Style for this episode.** Two light states, and the audience should feel the move at the TURN.

| | **the afternoon** (HOOK · ACT_1 · ACT_2 · ACT_3) | **the record** (ACT_4 · ACT_5 · ENDING) |
|---|---|---|
| light | overcast February daylight, sodium and fluorescent interiors, headlamps | flat north light, paper white, one lamp |
| palette | wet asphalt grey, orchard brown, supermarket white, tail-light red | manila, ink black, document ground, gold `#E5B53A` once per act |
| lens | 35mm, level, from a distance, people small in frame | 50–85mm, static, rooms with nobody in them |
| motion | drift and settle; the camera is always slightly late | almost still; the typography moves, the picture does not |

**Banned outright** (ship gate + owner directives): the left-to-right vertical sweep line, the
full-screen yellow or gold wash, plain zoom-and-pan on a still with nothing else happening, and the
slow orbiting glow. `animation_density` is a floor, not a target — it measures *not frozen*, not
*alive*. The measures that actually make this film move are: cut rate 3.8 s, the four accumulating
timestamps, the open line drawing itself, mask-reveal kinetic typography on every quotation, and
`Trail` motion blur on the three silence entries and exits.

**Depth.** Roughly 40% of stills carry a `DepthImage` parallax pass (the pipeline is integrated for
long form since EP29). The supermarket doorway, the valley floor and the empty shelter hall are the
three that most need it — they are rooms, and a room without parallax is a postcard.
