## 2. Description draft (EN · timestamps are PLACEHOLDERS — film not assembled)

```text
On a July morning in 1996, four people were murdered inside a family furniture store in Winona, Mississippi — and the crime has never been solved. Instead, one man, Curtis Flowers, a gospel singer with no criminal record, was tried for it six times by the same elected prosecutor, Doug Evans. Four convictions, four death sentences — every one thrown out. Two hung juries. Twenty-three years behind bars, most of them on death row at Parchman.

The case finally came apart in public: the state's star witness recanted his jailhouse-confession story on tape, and reporters assembled a quarter-century of jury records showing Evans' office struck Black jurors at nearly 4.5 times the rate of white ones. In 2019 the U.S. Supreme Court reversed the last conviction, 7–2, writing that "the numbers speak loudly." In 2020 Mississippi dropped every charge, with prejudice. Flowers received the state's statutory maximum — $500,000 — for nearly 23 lost years. Doug Evans was never charged and never disciplined; in 2022 his own district refused, 70–30, to make him a judge. The murders of Bertha Tardy, Carmen Rigby, Robert Golden, and 16-year-old Derrick Stewart remain officially unsolved.
```
