## 7. THE SECUREMENT — what the rules actually said

|---|---|---|---|

| LM-44 | MMA's own General Special Instructions §112-1 set the minimum by a **ten per cent plus two** formula. For a 72-car train that is 72 ÷ 10 + 2 = **9.2, rounded to nine**, and the nine included hand brakes on locomotives | VERBATIM | SRC-0001 |
| LM-45 | CROR 112(b) and MMA Safety Rule 9210(o) required the crew to **release all air brakes** and allow the slack to adjust "**by gravity, or by attempting to move the equipment slightly with reasonable locomotive force**" | VERBATIM QUOTE | SRC-0001 |

| LM-47 | The test was performed **with the independent brakes remaining active**, not with the air fully released | VERBATIM | SRC-0001 |

## What v002 changes about the film

Three things the design did not have and now must carry:

1. **ACT_2 gets its real number.** The comparison is no longer only "seven against seventeen to
   twenty-six". It is **seven against the railway's own rule of nine** (LM-44) — and **none of the
   seven was on a tank car** (LM-43). The company's own minimum was not met, by its own arithmetic,
   before any investigator arrived.
2. **ACT_3 gets its silence.** After the fire was out, **no decision to move the train or add brakes
   was documented** (LM-56), and MMA **could not reach anyone with locomotive engineering
   experience** (LM-53). The hour of falling pressure is not an absence of information. It is an
   absence of a decision.
3. **ACT_5 gets its ending.** The jury that acquitted **was never allowed to see the eighteen
   findings** (LM-72). The film has spent twenty minutes on a report that the trial could not hear.
   That is the closing argument, and it is a fact about evidence law, not a complaint about a jury
   (⛔-12).
