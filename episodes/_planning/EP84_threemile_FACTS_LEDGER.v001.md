> ## ⛔ R3 — WHAT THIS FILM MAY AND MAY NOT SAY
>
> 1. **The company pleaded GUILTY to ONE count and no contest to six. Four counts were dismissed
>    — including the only Title 18 false-statements count.** Met-Ed was **never convicted of any
>    Title 18 offence** (TM-305, TM-307). The film may not say "convicted of falsifying records"
>    without saying which count and which statute.
> 2. **The convicted conduct is the LEAK RATE test, and it happened BEFORE the accident.** The
>    charged period runs from **before 18 October 1978 up to and including 28 March 1979** —
>    it ends on the morning of the accident (TM-302). The film may not say the falsification
>    caused the meltdown. **No source establishes that.**
> 3. **The $1,000,000 is NOT a fine.** The fine was **$45,000**. The million was a condition of
>    the plea agreement, paid to the **Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency** for emergency
>    preparedness within 20 miles (TM-311, TM-312). "To clean up the plant" is false.
> 4. **One individual was convicted, in a SEPARATE case, of a DIFFERENT offence** — submitting
>    another person's work as his own on requalification examinations (TM-321). He is not named
>    in this film and his case is not the utility's case.
> 5. **No real-person likeness.** Not the operators, not the governor, not any resident.
> 6. **Nobody died at Three Mile Island.** The film states the health finding as the Commission
>    wrote it, with the Commission's own caveats — never as reassurance and never as alarm.

## E. THE TITLE DECISION — settled here

**Working title:** *The Utility at Three Mile Island Was Faking Its Safety Tests. Then Came
America's Worst Meltdown.*

**Verdict: the generalisation is supportable, and here is exactly why.**

1. The count Met-Ed **pleaded guilty to** charges knowingly continuing to use a procedure it had
   been **put on notice was inaccurate and meaningless**, **"in an effort to generate results
   which appeared to establish that reactor coolant leakage was within allowable limits"**
   (TM-203). That is a test being run to produce a passing answer. *Faking* is fair.
2. The statute of that count is **10 C.F.R. Part 50, Appendix B — Quality Assurance Criteria for
   Nuclear Power Plants** (TM-204), and the leakage limit it tested against was a **technical
   specification whose violation could shut the plant down** (TM-101, TM-103). It is a safety
   requirement, not a bookkeeping one.
3. The prosecutor said in open court that Count 2 **"goes to the very heart of the leak rate
   test"** (TM-314).

**BUT the script must carry the precision the title compresses**, and the ledger records it as a
binding constraint: the test is the **reactor coolant leak rate test**; the conduct ran **before
the accident**; and **no source in this ledger says the faking caused the meltdown.** The film
says what the record says: the test that was supposed to catch a leak had been meaningless for
months before the morning it would have mattered.
