## OP — ブランドタイトル [Bookends]

## ACT 1 — 普通の子供たち [S01 S02 S03 S04]

Luzerne County, Pennsylvania. Coal country, long after the coal. In the city of Wilkes-Barre stood a courthouse that was supposed to be the one place a kid who went wrong could be set right. [L]

The children who passed through it had done small things. A shoving match in a hallway. Twenty dollars of stolen makeup. A rude page online. The kind of thing that, almost anywhere else, ends with a warning and a ride home.

Here it began with a signature. A child was handed a form giving up the right to a lawyer, and — young, scared, taught to trust the adults in the room — signed it. Then came a man in a robe named Mark Ciavarella. [L: name/role]

Half of the children who signed that form left his courtroom in handcuffs, bound for a locked facility. In the rest of the state, that number was eight in a hundred. 〔CARD: 8.4%〕 [L: ~50% vs 8.4%]

And placement was not a slap on the wrist. It meant being taken that same day — cuffed, photographed, driven hours from home, and left to sleep behind a locked door for weeks or months. Missing school. Missing a parent's face. Learning to think of yourself as an inmate before you were old enough to drive.

It was fast, too. A minute. Two. A boy could be named a delinquent and walked out a side door before his mother rose from her seat.

And the parents believed it was justice, because who doesn't believe a judge? That belief — that a courtroom is a safe place to bring your child — was the first thing being sold.

## ACT 2 — 仕組み（金の流れ） [S05 S07 S08 S09 S15]

To see what was really happening, follow the money. It starts one floor up.

There were two judges. Ciavarella, in the juvenile court. And Michael Conahan, the president judge — the one who held the county's checkbook. [L: names/roles]

The county ran its own juvenile detention center, paid for with tax dollars. Conahan starved it of funding and moved it toward the door. [L: defunding] After that, when Ciavarella wanted a child gone, one place was waiting: a new, private, for-profit jail. Two of them, before it was done. [L: PA Child Care / Western PA Child Care]

A for-profit jail lives by one number. Empty beds cost money; full beds make it.

The beds were filled the only way they could be — one child at a time, by the judge down the hall.

A developer named Robert Mericle built those facilities. An attorney named Robert Powell owned and ran them. Across roughly three years, the two men passed about two point eight million dollars to the judges. 〔CARD: $2.8 MILLION〕 [L: Mericle/Powell/$2.8M/3yrs] They called it a finder's fee. A referral. Money, they said, for helping the places get built and kept busy.

Take the language away and it is very simple. A child walked in. A judge sent that child to a cell. A payment followed.

And the children who came without a lawyer? In that courtroom they appeared seven to eleven times more often than anywhere else in the state — 〔CARD: 7–11×〕 [L: 7–11x] because a child with no lawyer is a child who cannot fight back. Every hearing that felt too quick was not impatience on the bench. It was inventory.

## ACT 3 — 壊れた一家（人間の代償） [S06 S10 S11 S18]

A number this size goes silent. So stand in one house.

Ed Kenzakoski was seventeen. A wrestler, with a father who had a future picked out for him. He came before Ciavarella on a small charge, and he was sent away. [L: Kenzakoski, age/offense]

The boy who came home was not the boy who left. He carried it for years — the record, the lost time, the verdict passed on him at seventeen that he could never quite outrun. At twenty-three, he took his own life. [L: age/outcome — 事実のみ]

His mother is Sandy Fonzo. [L] And when Ciavarella walked down the steps of a federal courthouse years later — a defendant now — she was there. She met him in front of the cameras, grief turned to fury, and asked the man who had broken her son how he slept at night. He fixed his tie and looked past her. He had nothing. There is nothing to say.

Now set that against the size of it. Thousands of children told, at thirteen, at fifteen, that they were criminals — and, being children, believing it. A record that trailed them onto every job form and college application. Some never set it down. That is the part no ruling returns and no settlement buys back.

## ACT 4 — 暴かれる（司法が司法を裁く） [S12 S13 S14 S16 S17]

For years it held — because of who was doing the complaining. A kid with a record. A parent who "should have raised them better." Easy voices to wave off. And they were waved off, again and again.

Then one more mother picked up the phone — and called a small nonprofit in Philadelphia, the Juvenile Law Center. [L] Its lawyers pulled the files expecting a few heavy-handed sentences. They found a machine: hundreds of children, the same pattern each time — minutes in the room, no lawyer, straight to the same private beds. [L]

They took it to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court, and the court did a rare thing. It reached back into Ciavarella's courtroom and unmade it — throwing out thousands of adjudications, clearing the records of as many as several thousand children. 〔CARD: THOUSANDS VACATED〕 [L: ~2,251 vacated / up to ~4,000–6,500] The state, in plain terms, admitting that none of it should ever have counted.

But a cleared record is not a returned childhood. Behind each of those cases was a year, or two, or three, that the county had already spent — nights in a cell that no order could give back. The paperwork could be erased. The time could not.

Then the law turned on the men who had sold it. The FBI had been listening for some time. Michael Conahan pleaded guilty to racketeering; seventeen and a half years. Mark Ciavarella went to trial, was convicted of racketeering and corruption, and was given twenty-eight. 〔CARD: 28 YEARS〕 [L: 12/39, RICO, 28y / Conahan 17.5y] Later, a court ordered them to pay more than two hundred million dollars to the families they had harmed. [L: $200M+, 2022]

It was, at last, a kind of justice — and it forced a change: Pennsylvania moved to make sure a child in a courtroom actually has a lawyer at their side. [L: reform/right to counsel] But ask Sandy Fonzo if it was enough.

## ED — 余韻＋射程＋登録CTA [S19 S20 Bookends]

We like to believe a courtroom is the one room where the money stays outside the door. That the people with power are held to a higher line than the rest of us.

For thousands of children in one American county, that line had a price. It worked out to a few thousand dollars a head.

The judges are named and gone. One spent years in prison. The other, quietly, was granted an early release and walked back out into the world. [L: Conahan commuted 2024] The children keep their years. No one gives those back.

If this is a story that should be seen and not buried, subscribe — the next one opens the way this one did: an ordinary person, and a system that was never meant to reach them. Because the distance between a family like yours and a family like theirs is far shorter than we let ourselves believe.
