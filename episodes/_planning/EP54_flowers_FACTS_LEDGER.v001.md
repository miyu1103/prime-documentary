## ★ CONFIRMED FACTS

### The crime & the town (Act I)

- **F01 (H)** On the morning of **July 16, 1996**, four people were shot at **Tardy Furniture**, a family furniture store in downtown **Winona, Mississippi** (Montgomery County): owner **Bertha Tardy, 59**; bookkeeper/employee **Carmen Rigby, 45**; employee **Robert Golden, 42**; and **Derrick "Bobo" Stewart, 16**, a new hire. Each victim was shot in the head. ⚠ State this once, plainly; no re-creation, no scene detail beyond this. *(Wikipedia; APM In the Dark "What happened that morning"; YourTango/AP recap.)*
- **F02 (H · ⚠)** Bertha Tardy phoned retired employee **Sam Jones Jr.** around **9:00 a.m.** to come help train new employees; Jones arrived about an hour later and **discovered the scene**. Three victims were already dead; **Derrick Stewart was still alive**, was rushed to the hospital, and **died on July 22, 1996**. ⚠ Treat Stewart's six days with dignity — a 16-year-old who fought and lost; no medical detail. *(APM In the Dark; YourTango/AP recap.)*
- **F03 (M · one-source lineage)** Roughly **$300–$400 was missing** from the store cash drawer (reporting commonly says about $400). SOFT-CITE as "a few hundred dollars." *(Wikipedia.)*
- **F04 (H)** Winona is a small town in central Mississippi — population roughly **5,000**, split roughly **half Black, half white**. SOFT-CITE the split as "roughly half." *(In the Dark series framing; Mississippi Free Press.)*
- **F05 (H)** **Curtis Flowers** — born **May 29, 1970**, age **26** at the time — was a local man who **sang gospel** with his family's group and had **no prior criminal record**. *(Wikipedia; In the Dark; 60 Minutes 2021 framing. State the no-record point plainly; it is uncontested in reporting.)*
- **F06 (H)** Flowers had worked briefly at Tardy Furniture and had been **fired about two weeks (13 days) before the murders**; reporting says he owed Bertha Tardy roughly **$30** from a paycheck advance. The prosecution's motive theory: **retaliation for being fired**. SOFT-CITE the $30 figure; frame motive as "the state's theory." *(Wikipedia; YourTango/AP.)*
- **F07 (H)** **District Attorney Doug Evans** — the elected prosecutor of Mississippi's Fifth Circuit Court District — led the case from the beginning and **personally prosecuted all six trials**. Flowers became a suspect almost immediately after investigators learned he had been fired from the store. *(Wikipedia; DPIC; Mississippi Today.)*
- **F08 (H)** Flowers was **arrested in January 1997** and remained **continuously behind bars from January 1997 until December 2019 — nearly 23 years**, **most of that time on death row at Parchman** (Mississippi State Penitentiary). ⚠ Do NOT assert an exact death-row year count (e.g., "13 years") — reporting verified this session says "most of it on death row"; hedge as "most of those years" / "more than two decades behind bars." *(Wikipedia; Mississippi Today; Revolt/AP compensation reporting.)*

### The case against him — paper-thin (Act I)

- **F09 (H)** **No murder weapon was ever found.** The state's theory: a **.380-caliber pistol** was stolen that morning from the car of **Doyle Simpson** (parked at the garment plant where Simpson worked), and the fatal bullets matched that gun. *(Wikipedia; In the Dark.)*
- **F10 (M)** The ballistics link was built by comparing crime-scene projectiles to bullets said to have been fired earlier from Simpson's gun and recovered elsewhere; **independent firearms experts consulted by In the Dark called the certainty of the match unsupportable** ("junk science" framing is In the Dark's, attribute it). SOFT-CITE mechanics; attribute doubts to the experts. *(In the Dark S2.)*
- **F11 (M)** **Route witnesses**: months after the murders — after reward money was publicized — witnesses emerged saying they had seen Flowers that morning walking the route from his home toward the garment plant and then toward Tardy Furniture. **In the Dark found the route case collapsed under scrutiny — key route witnesses recanted or gave contradictory accounts.** SOFT-CITE; attribute recantations to In the Dark's on-record interviews. Do not name individual route witnesses in narration. *(In the Dark S2; Wikipedia.)*
- **F12 (M)** **Gunshot residue**: a single particle consistent with gunshot residue was reported on the back of Flowers' hand, swabbed hours after the murders; experts told In the Dark a lone particle hours later is effectively meaningless (transfer/contamination). SOFT-CITE as "a single microscopic particle… experts say it proves nothing." *(In the Dark S2; Wikipedia's "gunpowder residue" is the state's framing.)*
- **F13 (M)** A **bloody shoeprint** at the scene was matched by the state to a **size 10½ Fila Grant Hill** shoe style, which the state connected to Flowers; the connection (whose shoes, where found) was contested and undermined in later reporting. SOFT-CITE: "a shoeprint in a common sneaker style and size." *(Wikipedia; In the Dark.)*
- **F14 (M)** Reporting notes roughly **$235 found in Flowers' headboard** offered by the state as suspicious; defense countered it was ordinary cash. SOFT-CITE or omit. *(Wikipedia.)*

### Six trials — the machine (Acts II–III)

- **F15 (H)** **Trial 1 — October 1997**, venue moved to **Lee County (Tupelo)**; Flowers tried for the murder of **Bertha Tardy only**. Jury: **all white**. Verdict: guilty; **sentence: death**. **Reversed by the Mississippi Supreme Court in 2000** (*Flowers I*, 773 So. 2d 309) for **prosecutorial misconduct** — including arguing facts not in evidence. ★ NOTE: the topic brief's "Trial 1 = Bobo Stewart count" was WRONG; Trial 1 was the Tardy count (Trial 2 was the Stewart count). *(FindLaw/Miss. S. Ct. record; Wikipedia; SCOTUS opinion history section.)*
- **F16 (H)** **Trial 2 — March 1999**, venue **Harrison County (Gulf Coast)**; tried for the murder of **Derrick Stewart only**. Jury: **11 white, 1 Black**. Guilty; **death**. **Reversed 2003** (*Flowers II*, 842 So. 2d 531) — again for **prosecutorial misconduct**. *(FindLaw; Wikipedia; SCOTUS opinion.)*
- **F17 (H)** **Trial 3 — February 2004**, **Montgomery County (Winona)**; tried for **all four murders**. Jury: **11 white, 1 Black**. Guilty; **death**. **Reversed 2007** (*Flowers III*, 947 So. 2d 910) for a **Batson violation** — racially discriminatory jury selection by the state. The Mississippi Supreme Court wrote (VERIFIED verbatim): **"The instant case presents us with as strong a prima facie case of racial discrimination as we have ever seen in the context of a Batson challenge."** *(Miss. S. Ct. via SCOTUS opinion; Wikipedia.)*
- **F18 (H)** **Trial 4 — 2007**, Montgomery County, all four counts. Jury: **7 white, 5 Black**. **Hung jury — deadlocked 7–5**: reporting states the **five Black jurors voted to acquit, the seven white jurors to convict**. Mistrial. *(Friends of Justice; Wikipedia; In the Dark.)*
- **F19 (H)** **Trial 5 — 2008**, all four counts. Jury: **9 white, 3 Black**. **Hung jury** — the deadlock came down to a **lone holdout against conviction, juror James Bibbs (Black)**. *(Friends of Justice; The Mississippi Link; Wikipedia.)*
- **F20 (H · the astonishing beat)** When trial 5 hung, **Bibbs was handcuffed in the courtroom and charged with perjury** — a prosecution pursued by **Evans** before Judge **Joseph Loper**; Evans and Loper were later recused from the Bibbs matter and the **Mississippi Attorney General's office dropped the charge (October 2009)**. State exactly this — juror charged, charge dropped — no speculation about motive beyond the record. *(The Mississippi Link "Charges dismissed against… Flowers' juror"; Friends of Justice, Oct 2009.)*
- **F21 (H)** **Trial 6 — June 2010**, Montgomery County (Winona), all four counts. Jury: **11 white, 1 Black**. Guilty on four counts of capital murder — reporting: after about **30 minutes of deliberation** (SOFT-CITE the minutes) — and **sentenced to death**. This fourth death sentence is the one the U.S. Supreme Court would strike down nine years later. *(Wikipedia; Jackson Free Press 2010.)*
- **F22 (H)** Across the six trials: **4 convictions + death sentences (trials 1, 2, 3, 6) — every one overturned — and 2 hung juries (trials 4, 5)**. No verdict against Curtis Flowers ever survived review. *(All sources; SCOTUS opinion history.)*

### In the Dark & the numbers (Act III–IV)

- **F28 (H)** **APM Reports' podcast In the Dark, Season 2 (launched May 2018)** — a team led by **Madeleine Baran** spent about a year reporting from Winona — systematically dismantled the case: the gun/route chain, the ballistics certainty, the informants, and the jury-selection record. *(APM Reports; Wikipedia.)*
- **F29 (H)** **The strike study**: APM Reports compiled data on **more than 6,700 jurors across 225 trials (1992–2017)** in Evans' district and found his office **struck Black prospective jurors at nearly 4½ times the rate of white ones** — striking **50% of eligible Black jurors vs 11% of eligible whites** — a disparity that persisted even controlling for race-neutral answers. *(APM Reports strike-study page + methodology PDF; DPIC.)*

### SCOTUS & the fall (Act IV — stack LAST)

- **F31 (H)** ***Flowers v. Mississippi*, decided June 21, 2019, 7–2** — majority by **Justice Brett Kavanaugh**; **Thomas dissented, joined in large part by Gorsuch**. Holding: the trial-6 conviction was reversed because Evans' peremptory strike of a Black prospective juror violated **Batson v. Kentucky** — the Constitution's bar on race discrimination in jury selection. *(SCOTUS slip op. via Cornell LII; Wikipedia.)*
- **F32 (H · VERIFIED verbatim, the four pillars)** From Kavanaugh's opinion:
  1. **"The State's relentless, determined effort to rid the jury of black individuals strongly suggests that the State wanted to try Flowers before a jury with as few black jurors as possible, and ideally before an all-white jury."**
  2. **"In the six trials combined, the State employed its peremptory challenges to strike 41 of the 42 black prospective jurors that it could have struck — a statistic that the State acknowledged at oral argument in this Court."**
  3. **"The numbers speak loudly."** (Followed by: over the first four trials there were 36 Black prospective jurors the state could have struck — **"The State tried to strike all 36."**)
  4. **"Equal justice under law requires a criminal trial free of racial discrimination in the jury selection process."**
  *(Cornell LII full text.)*
- **F33 (H)** Trial-6 strike facts found by the Court: the state **struck five of the six Black prospective jurors**; it asked the five struck Black jurors **a total of 145 questions** and the 11 seated white jurors **a total of 12**. *(SCOTUS slip op.)*
- **F34 (H)** **December 16, 2019**: Circuit Judge **Joseph Loper** — the same judge who had presided over the later trials — **granted Flowers bail ($250,000)** for the first time in 23 years; Flowers left jail wearing an ankle monitor. - **F35 (H)** **January 2020**: Evans **recused himself** from the case; it passed to **Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch's office**. **September 4, 2020**: the AG's office **dropped all charges — dismissed WITH PREJUDICE** — meaning Curtis Flowers can never be tried for this again. *(DPIC; NPR/AP; APM.)*
- **F36 (H)** **March 2021**: Circuit Judge **George Mitchell** ordered the State of Mississippi to pay Flowers **$500,000 — the statutory maximum** ($50,000 per year of wrongful imprisonment, capped at 10 years), paid **$50,000 a year for 10 years**. The cap means he is compensated for **less than half** the years he lost. *(APM Reports; Revolt/AP; localmemphis/AP.)*
- **F37 (H)** **September 2021**: Flowers filed a **federal civil rights lawsuit against Doug Evans** (and investigators) for engineering his wrongful prosecution; the suit was **settled in 2023; terms undisclosed**. ### Legal-framing facts (for the cold open — precision required)

- **F42 (H)** The Double Jeopardy Clause does **not** bar retrial after a conviction is **reversed on appeal** or after a **hung jury** — which is the legal mechanism that made six trials possible. The cold open's "tried six times" hook MUST be framed honestly: the protection most Americans assume ("you can't be tried twice") has this exception, and Evans drove through it six times. Never claim double jeopardy was violated. *(Black-letter law; SCOTUS opinion recounts the procedural history without any double-jeopardy bar.)*
- **F43 (H)** *Batson v. Kentucky* (1986): the Constitution forbids striking jurors because of race; a "peremptory strike" is a strike given without stated cause. One sentence of doctrine max — this is a story film, not an explainer.
