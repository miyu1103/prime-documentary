## 0. THE SELECTION IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The pools contain 70 candidates and the measurement passes contain one decisive finding: **the protagonist, not the subject, is the conversion variable** — identical doctrine converts at 0.5–0.8% CTR with a convict-seeking-exoneration protagonist and at 3–18× channel median with a landowner, a driver, a mechanic or a grandmother. Twenty-four of the 36 US candidates are convict-seeking-exoneration. **This slate takes zero of them.** The second binding fact is the owner's 30-minute mandate colliding head-on with our own analytics, which rank 24–36 min *below* 9–13 min on watch-minutes per slot once each band's single outlier is removed (58.0 vs 67.7) and permit the long band only for premises carrying their own external demand. I therefore promoted that permission from a preference to a **hard co-filter**: a finalist had to survive *both* a 6–8 beat / 2-reversal structural test *and* an independently measured demand signal. Four otherwise-superb candidates died on the first test and are named in §3; the immigration lane and the whole convict-protagonist family died on the second and third. What survives is three films with ordinary-citizen protagonists, three different villain shapes, three different harms, three verified or primary-anchored money quotes, zero confession-engine films, and — for the first time in the EP50s — a slate whose weakest packaging field is a loss verb rather than a protagonist.
