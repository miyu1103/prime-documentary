Compiled 2026-08-24. Primary source downloaded and read: NIST NCSTAR 2 Volume 1, 252 pages.

## 1 · The building

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-001 | The Station was a **single-storey, wood-framed, unsprinklered** commercial building of about **412 square metres (4,484 square feet)** | 53 |
| ST-002 | **Three emergency exits were available to patrons**: the front main entrance on the north, a single door on the west near the performance platform, and a single door on the east side of the main bar | 22 |
| ST-003 | **A fourth door, in the kitchen, was not considered accessible to patrons** | 22 |
| ST-004 | **The front main entrance was limited by the single doorway inside the vestibule — not by the double doors visible from outside** | 22 |
| ST-005 | Inside the double doors of the main entrance was a vestibule with a single doorway | 30 |
| ST-006 | **The wood structure and panelling contained over 95 percent of the fuel load.** Once most of the foam was consumed, the fire became an ordinary wood-frame building fire | 20 |

**ST-004 is the spine of this film.** From the car park it is a pair of doors. One step inside, it
is one door.

## 2 · The occupant limit

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-101 | The number of people in the building was reported by news media as **between 440 and 458** | 22 |
| ST-102 | **Under the 2003 model codes, the occupant limit for a building like The Station would have been around 420** | 22 |
| ST-103 | A 1981 application under the fire safety code identified an occupant load of **225** for the building as then used | 189 |
| ST-104 | A West Warwick Fire Department memorandum of **30 December 1999** identified the occupant load as **253** | 189 |
| ST-105 | **That same memorandum granted an allowance to increase the occupancy to 317** by removing tables and chairs from three lounge areas and providing standing room only | 189 |
| ST-106 | The Attorney General's office was reported as stating that **458 people** were inside that evening | 189 |
| ST-107 | NIST also counted occupants from a television crew's video of the club that night; **144 occupants are visible across four still frames**, and the report is explicit that the timing of that sequence relative to the concert is unknown | 190 |

**Note the staircase in the paperwork:** 225, then 253, then 317, against a code figure of about
420, on a night with 440 to 458 people inside.

## 3 · The foam, and the test NIST ran afterwards

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-201 | The fire began when **pyrotechnics used during a band's performance ignited polyurethane foam** lining portions of the walls and ceiling of the platform | 20, 29 |
| ST-202 | The hazard was created by the close proximity of a **high-temperature source and a large amount of material with low ignition energy and a high flame-spread rate** | 20 |
| ST-203 | **A non-fire-retarded foam sample bought by NIST ignited in less than 15 seconds** when exposed to a pyrotechnic device arranged as on the club's platform | 21 |
| ST-204 | **A fire-retarded polyurethane foam bought by NIST, exposed the same way, did not ignite at all** | 21 |
| ST-205 | **With no foam present, the wood panelling did not ignite either** | 21 |
| ST-206 | A second material contributed: a **foam plastic thermal insulation installed in the stud space with no fire-resistant barrier** on the interior side of the drummer's alcove walls | 21 |

**ST-203 and ST-204 together are the whole material argument.** Same device, same arrangement, two
foams. Fifteen seconds, or nothing at all.

## 4 · The ninety seconds

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-301 | **The first patrons recognised the danger about 24 seconds after ignition** | 22 |
| ST-302 | The bulk of the crowd began to evacuate shortly after that, **around the time the band stopped playing — about 30 seconds** | 22 |
| ST-303 | **Smoke was visible in the exit doorways in a little more than one minute** | 20, 29 |
| ST-304 | Occupants were leaving the building in a steady stream at **1 minute 11 seconds** | 52 |
| ST-305 | **At about 1 minute 30 seconds the front exit became blocked with people**, and occupants could be seen breaking windows and escaping from the pool room and sun room | 52 |
| ST-306 | **The crowd crush at the front exit was captured on video at 1 minute 42 seconds** | 52 |
| ST-307 | **Flames were observed breaking through a portion of the roof in less than five minutes** | 20, 29 |
| ST-308 | The latest recorded escape from inside the main bar, through a window, was at **4 minutes 8 seconds** | 52 |
| ST-309 | **People stuck in the front entrance are seen escaping as late as five and a half minutes** into the fire, just before the fire department ran its first hose line | 52 |
| ST-310 | Simulation: **the dance floor and adjacent areas reach untenable temperatures within 90 seconds of ignition**. The sun room and dart room within another 10 seconds | 159 |
| ST-311 | In the sun room next to the entranceway — **a place where many of the dead were found** — temperatures exceeded the tenability threshold within 90 seconds, rising from ambient to flame temperatures of around 1,000°C **in ten seconds** | 160 |
| ST-312 | Tenability is defined as the point at which temperature exceeds 120°C, heat flux exceeds 2.5 kW/m², or oxygen falls below 12 percent | 112 |

## 5 · Where they went

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-401 | **Between 56 and 66 percent of the occupants appear to have attempted to leave through the single main entrance at the front. Many were unsuccessful** | 22 |
| ST-402 | The windows of the main bar room and the sun room **became the secondary routes of escape once the main entrance blocked** | 22 |

## 6 · The counterfactuals NIST modelled

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-501 | In the unsprinklered experiment, **the first sprinkler above the platform would have activated 33 seconds after ignition** | 112 |
| ST-502 | **Doubling the width of the entrance door** in the evacuation model brought the number of people still inside at 90 seconds **back down to the range of the baseline scenario** | 206 |
| ST-503 | The crowd crush had not occurred before 90 seconds, and the front door remained open through those first 90 seconds | 206 |

## 7 · What NIST concluded and asked for

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| ST-601 | **One hundred people lost their lives in the fire** | 20, 29 |
| ST-602 | The three direct contributors NIST names: **(1) the hazardous mix of building contents, (2) the inadequate capability to suppress the fire during its early stage of growth, and (3) the inability of the exits to handle all of the occupants in the short time available for such a fast-growing fire** | 20 |
| ST-603 | **Both 2003 editions of the major model building and fire codes already had provisions that address these problems** | 20 |
| ST-604 | NIST recommends that pyrotechnic devices **be banned from indoor use in new and existing nightclubs not equipped with a compliant automatic sprinkler system** | 25 |
| ST-605 | NIST recommends that **the factor of safety for determining occupancy limits** of all new and existing nightclubs be increased in the model codes | 25 |
| ST-606 | NIST records the behaviours that compromise a protective system: installing decorations that exceed flame-spread provisions, exposing the building to strong ignition sources, **exceeding the posted occupancy limits, temporarily blocking an exit, and disabling sprinklers for maintenance** | 27, 238 |
| ST-607 | NIST states the need to **understand better how crowds behave in emergencies, to pinpoint the factors that lead to crowd crush**, and to change building design to reduce the possibility | 28 |
