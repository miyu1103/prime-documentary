## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **The evidence that cleared her existed on the first morning. Everything that happened afterwards
> happened anyway.**

Not "the system failed". Not "bite-mark science is unreliable". Those are conclusions, and the film
does not need to reach for them — the record reaches them by itself. The idea is narrower and colder:
**nothing new was ever found against her.** Each accusation was built out of a person who later took
it back, and the one piece of physical evidence was revised on request by a man who looked at nothing.

The audience should leave able to say what the film never says out loud: *at no point did anyone learn
anything.*

## 2. WHAT THE FILM IS ABOUT, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

| It is about | It is not about |
|---|---|
| eleven springs that happened without her | courtrooms, cells, gavels, bars |
| a place carrying on | forensic odontology as a subject |
| how a case is assembled out of retractions | who killed Curtis Haith (⛔-01) |
| the first morning, and 2016 | the prosecutor as a villain (⛔-06) |

**The register decision is measured, not aesthetic.** Cross-episode burn: courtroom 23/24, cell 20/24,
prison 18/24, handcuffs 7/7 — after 60 episodes the legal shelf is spent. And demand measured the same
day: "innocent man released after decades" medians 862k views; "forensic expert wrong testimony"
medians 19k; "prosecutor hid evidence" medians 1,068. **The audience wants the years, not the
mechanism.** Both measurements point the same way.

## 3. THE OPEN LOOP (row 16)

**Opened at 0:00–0:08. Closed at 28:40.**

Cold open states: on the morning of 27 January 2001, a young woman told police where she had been and
how she got the marks on her face and foot. *Fifteen years later, an expert looked at the photographs
taken that morning and said she had been telling the truth.* The question held for thirty minutes:
**what happened in between, and why did none of it require anything new?**

The loop closes on CW-35 — the 2016 expert review — not on the dismissal. The dismissal is the outcome;
the review is the answer.

## 4. THE FOUR MOTIFS

Each recurs at least three times, changed each time.

**A. The first morning.** Her clothes, the mud, the injury to her foot, the account she gave. Returns
at the arrest (2004), at the trial (2006), and last at CW-35, where the same photographs are examined
and match the same account. *It never changed. Everything around it did.*

**B. Someone takes it back.** Beal on the stand (CW-26). Stenger in post-conviction (CW-32). The
bite-mark expert disavowing his own testimony (CW-33). Three retractions, spaced across the film,
each one landing later and costing more.

**C. The same place, another season.** The field, the creek, the porch, the street. Eleven cycles.
Never remarked on in narration — the pictures do it.

**D. Nothing new.** Spoken plainly at three points: the expert revising the timing without reviewing
additional evidence (CW-16); the case proceeding "despite the conflicting statements" (CW-23); the
investigation continuing after dismissal (CW-28).

## 6. THE BEAT MAP

`section_vocabulary` is fixed by the contract: HOOK, OP, ACT_1…ACT_5, ENDING.

### HOOK — 0:00–0:45
Flash-forward montage, ~2 s cuts, written LAST. The first morning; a season turning; a courtroom door
closing (audio only); the 2016 line. Ends on the question. **Payoff verified before lock.**

### OP — 0:45–1:10
Bookends. Title.

### ACT_1 — THE FIRST MORNING · 1:10–6:30
Connellsville, January 2001. Haith is found (CW-01, no depiction). Vernon comes to the scene (CW-02).
A dozen people had been through the apartment (CW-04). Officers reach Weimer's house; she is still in
last night's clothes (CW-05). She explains the marks and where she was; her cousin, her sisters and
Gibson confirm it (CW-06, CW-07).
**Act turn (3:10):** the DNA comes back. The blood is Gibson's. Nothing at the scene matches her. The
scene profile is male (CW-08).
Ends on the town going quiet. Twenty months begin.

### ACT_2 — TWENTY MONTHS · 6:30–12:00
Time passes and nothing is found. Then October 2002: Beal (CW-09). His account contains a detail the
DNA has already ruled out (CW-10). The Cold Case Squad arrives (CW-11). The autopsy photographs are
reviewed and someone sees a bite mark (CW-12).
**Act turn (9:30):** the dentist concludes Gibson bit him — then, given Weimer's impressions, says she
cannot identify whose teeth made the mark (CW-13).

### ACT_3 — NOTHING NEW · 12:00–18:00
The second expert. What he was given: Beal's statement, the photographs, the impressions — **the
informant's story first** (CW-14). His conclusion: Weimer.
Then the timing is questioned. Vernon directs officers to look into it. The expert is asked to update
his opinion (CW-15).
**Act turn (15:00):** he revises it to seven-to-ten minutes before death, **without reviewing
additional evidence** (CW-16). Held silence.
Beal changes his story again — "a black man named Lonnie" (CW-17) — and the man was incarcerated at the
time (CW-18). Verbatim on screen: *"Despite these puzzling changes to his story, Beal remained a key
witness."* (CW-19)
Blair, from prison, with a statement that contradicts his own account (CW-20, CW-21). A dive team
searches a pond. Stenger's attorney says his client did not write it (CW-22).

### ACT_4 — ELEVEN SPRINGS · 18:00–24:00
December 2003: the complaint is prepared "despite the conflicting statements", and approved (CW-23).
January 2004: arrest, three years on (CW-24).
**19:40 — the case falls apart almost immediately.** Beal recants on the stand: *"kind of like coaxed
me along on how to do it."* Charges dismissed. She goes home (CW-25, CW-26, CW-27).
**21:00 — "Nevertheless, investigators continued to focus their efforts on Weimer."** (CW-28)
July 2004: Stenger's offer — implication in exchange for a lighter sentence (CW-29). Second arrest.
7 April 2006: convicted of third-degree murder and conspiracy. **Fifteen to thirty years** (CW-30).
**Then the film stops talking.** The remaining ~2:30 of this act is the seasons: the same field, the
same creek, the same porch, the same street, the same window, nine years of them. Narration is minimal
— dates only, or nothing. **This is the act the archive could not supply and the AI stills exist for.**

### ACT_5 — WHAT CAME OUT · 24:00–28:00
1 October 2015: the convictions are vacated (CW-31).
Stenger: he knew nothing, and police walked him through it (CW-32).
The bite-mark expert disavows his own testimony — *"junk science"*, **his words** (CW-33).
The letters in the files: informants who swore they had no deals had asked for them (CW-34).
27 June 2016: **dropped with prejudice** (CW-36).

### ENDING — 28:00–30:00
**28:40, the loop closes.** In 2016 an expert reviewed the autopsy report and the photographs of her
injuries taken on the morning of 27 January 2001, and concluded they were consistent with the account
she gave that morning (CW-35).
Then: *more than eleven years* (CW-37).
Then what the film will not say: who killed Curtis Haith is not established by any of this (⛔-01), and
the film says so plainly rather than implying an answer.
Last image: the road out of town under an overcast sky.

## 7. REGISTER RULES FOR THE WRITER

1. **No emotion commands.** Never "imagine", "picture this", "sit with that". The facts carry it.
   Mechanically enforced by `check_script_craft.py`.
2. **Attribute allegations.** The opinion recites Weimer's pleaded allegations and says so (CW-42).
   Anything about intent is "Weimer alleged". ⛔-06.
3. **Let the verbatims land alone.** CW-19, CW-25, CW-26, CW-28, CW-33, CW-36 are the film's spine.
   Each gets its own beat and its own silence. Do not paraphrase them and do not stack two together.
4. **Dates are the rhythm.** January 2001. October 2002. December 2003. January 2004. July 2004.
   April 2006. October 2015. June 2016. Said plainly, they do the work of a clock.
5. **The mechanism gets one sentence, then it is shown.** Absolute vs qualified immunity, third-degree
   murder, dismissal with prejudice — one clean line each, never a paragraph.
6. **Never say the film's thesis out loud.** "Nothing new was ever found" is the shape, not a line.
