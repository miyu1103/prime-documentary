## ACT I — THE FIRST MORNING (1:10–6:30)

Connellsville sits in Fayette County, in the south-west corner of Pennsylvania, where the hills start to fold. Coal country, once. Then not.

In the early hours of the twenty-seventh of January, officers from the Connellsville Police Department went to the apartment of a man named Curtis Haith.

They found him outside on the pavement. He had been beaten. He had been shot in the face.

He was twenty-two years old.

At the officers' request, the District Attorney came to the apartment herself. Her name was Nancy Vernon. She took part in the investigation and helped direct it, that morning and for years afterwards.

Inside, the search recovered DNA samples. It also recovered a significant amount of drug-related evidence.

The neighbours told police that Haith had been out that night, and that a dozen or more people had passed through his apartment between the evening of the twenty-sixth and the morning of the twenty-seventh.

So the officers began working through the list. Everyone who had been at those parties. One after another.

One of them was a twenty-three-year-old woman called Crystal Weimer.

⟨BEAT⟩

When they arrived at her house that morning, she was still wearing the clothes she had worn the night before.

She had a minor injury to her face. She had a minor injury to her foot. There was what looked like mud on her clothes, and what looked like blood.

She told them her account without being asked twice.

She had been at the party. She had given Haith a ride and dropped him at another party. After that she had gone to the housing community where her mother and her sisters lived, and stayed there.

Her cousin confirmed it. Her sisters confirmed it. Michael Gibson, the man she was seeing, confirmed it.

She explained the injuries too. The foot was from horseplaying with Gibson a few days earlier. The eye, and the blood on the shirt, were from a fight with Gibson.

That was the account she gave on the twenty-seventh of January, two thousand and one.

Hold on to it. It does not change once in the next fifteen years.

【OST: 27 JANUARY 2001】

⟨BEAT⟩

Then the laboratory results came back.

The blood on Crystal Weimer's clothes was Michael Gibson's blood. Exactly as she had said.

None of the DNA collected from the crime scene matched her. Not one sample.

And the samples that were there suggested something else. They suggested a male profile.

⟨HELD⟩

That is the whole of the physical evidence, on the twenty-seventh of January, two thousand and one.

It points away from her. It does not point at her once.

⟨BEAT⟩

And then nothing happened.

The file stayed open. The investigation did not close and it did not move. Winter went. Spring came. The town did what towns do.

Nobody was charged. Nobody was cleared, either. Curtis Haith's family waited.

Twenty months went by like that.

## ACT II — TWENTY MONTHS (6:30–12:00)

In October of two thousand and two, a man named Thomas Beal went to the police.

Weimer had dated Beal before she was seeing Gibson. What he told officers was that Weimer and Gibson had killed Curtis Haith.

According to Beal, Weimer had told him something specific. She had told him that the blood on her clothes was Haith's.

The DNA had already established, twenty months earlier, that the blood on her clothes was Gibson's.

So the first statement against her contained a fact that the evidence had already ruled out.

⟨HELD⟩

Beal was not set aside. The investigation now had a direction.

Around the same time, the Pennsylvania State Police Cold Case Squad began to assist. New people. The same file.

One of them went back through the autopsy photographs of Curtis Haith's body. And on his hand, she saw something she believed was a bite mark.

【OST: OCTOBER 2002】

⟨BEAT⟩

A dentist in Fayette County was asked to analyse the injury.

She looked at it. She compared it against teeth impressions. And she reached a conclusion.

Her conclusion was that Michael Gibson had bitten Curtis Haith.

Then she was given impressions of Crystal Weimer's teeth as well, and asked to look again.

After that comparison, she reported that she could not identify which set of teeth had caused the mark.

⟨HELD⟩

That is where the dentist stopped. She said she could not tell.

So a different expert was brought in.

⟨BEAT⟩

Here is what that expert was given to work with.

Photographs of the injury to Haith's hand. Teeth impressions from Gibson and from Weimer.

And Thomas Beal's statement.

He read the accusation, and then he examined the evidence.

His conclusion was that the bite mark matched Crystal Weimer.

⟨HELD⟩

One dentist had said Gibson. Then she had said she could not tell. The next expert said Weimer, and he had been given the name first.

That is the physical case. There is nothing else in it.

## ACT III — NOTHING NEW (12:00–18:00)

A problem surfaced with the bite mark, and it was a serious one.

Nobody could say when it had happened.

A bite on a hand could have been left hours before a man died. It could have been left days before. If it was days before, it proved nothing about the night of the murder at all.

So the question of timing was put back to the investigation. Vernon directed officers to look into it, and the expert was asked to update his opinion.

He did.

He determined that the bite had occurred seven to ten minutes before Curtis Haith's death.

⟨HELD⟩

He reached that conclusion without reviewing any additional evidence.

⟨HELD⟩

Nothing new had been found. Nothing new had been tested. The same photographs, the same impressions, the same man. A question was asked, and the answer moved.

Seven to ten minutes. Close enough to put her there at the moment he died.

【OST: WITHOUT REVIEWING ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE】

⟨BEAT⟩

Meanwhile investigators went back to Thomas Beal.

He had a new version.

He now said that a third person had taken part in the murder. A man he called Lonnie.

Officers investigated the name, at the direction of the police and of Vernon. They established what had happened to Lonnie.

At the time Curtis Haith was killed, Lonnie was incarcerated.

⟨HELD⟩

The court that later reviewed all of this wrote one sentence about what followed.

⟨HELD⟩
"Despite these puzzling changes to his story, Beal remained a key witness."

⟨BEAT⟩

In August of two thousand and three — two and a half years after the murder now — a prisoner named Conrad Blair contacted the police.

Blair said that a fellow inmate, Joseph Stenger, had confessed to being involved in Haith's death.

Vernon and a Connellsville officer went to interview him. Blair told them that Stenger, Weimer and Beal had killed Curtis Haith.

He also handed over a written statement. He said Stenger had written it.

The statement did not say what Blair said it said. In the statement, Stenger was not involved in the killing at all. He had only helped dispose of evidence, in a pond.

⟨HELD⟩

On the strength of that, Vernon helped assemble a dive team, and they searched the pond.

Vernon and several officers also met with Stenger's attorney.

The attorney told them his client had not written the statement.

⟨HELD⟩

Three accusations now. Beal, who had described blood the DNA had already ruled out, and then added a man who was in prison. Blair, whose document contradicted him. Stenger, who had not written what was attributed to him.

None of it agreed with any of the rest of it.

## ACT IV — ELEVEN SPRINGS (18:00–24:00)

In late December of two thousand and three, officers prepared a criminal complaint charging Crystal Weimer with the murder of Curtis Haith.

The court that reviewed the case put it in five words. The complaint was prepared "despite the conflicting statements."

Nancy Vernon approved it.

In January of two thousand and four, three years after Haith died, Crystal Weimer was arrested.

⟨BEAT⟩

The case against her fell apart almost immediately.

At the preliminary hearing, the Commonwealth called Thomas Beal to the stand as a witness of fact.

Under oath, Beal took it all back.

⟨HELD⟩
He testified that an officer had — his words — "kind of like coaxed me along on how to do it."

The judge dismissed the charges. Crystal Weimer went home.

⟨HELD⟩

She was home for six months.

⟨HELD⟩
"Nevertheless, investigators continued to focus their efforts on Weimer."

⟨BEAT⟩

In July of two thousand and four, Joseph Stenger made an offer.

He told police he would implicate Weimer in the murder. In exchange, he wanted a lighter sentence on convictions of his own, in an unrelated matter.

On the strength of that statement, officers arrested Crystal Weimer for a second time and charged her again.

This time it went to trial.

On the seventh of April, two thousand and six, a jury in Fayette County convicted her of third-degree murder and of conspiracy to commit murder.

She was sentenced to fifteen to thirty years.

## ACT V — WHAT CAME OUT (24:00–28:00)

On the first of October, two thousand and fifteen, a judge vacated Crystal Weimer's convictions and granted her a new trial.

That decision opened the file to people who had not seen it before. And over the next nine months, one piece at a time, the case came apart in public.

⟨BEAT⟩

Joseph Stenger recanted.

He conceded that he had known nothing about Curtis Haith's murder. And he said that police had walked him through his testimony.

⟨BEAT⟩

The bite-mark expert disavowed what he had told the jury.

The opinion he had given — that the mark on Curtis Haith's hand was Crystal Weimer's — he now described in two words.

⟨HELD⟩
"Junk science."

⟨HELD⟩

That is not this film's assessment. That is the man who gave the evidence, describing the evidence he gave.

⟨BEAT⟩

Then Weimer's lawyers went through the District Attorney's files, and they found letters.

The letters were from jailhouse informants. The same informants who had testified against her at trial.

At trial, those witnesses had told the jury that they were not receiving anything in exchange for their testimony.

The letters showed that they had asked for deals. And that they may have received them.

⟨BEAT⟩

And an expert was asked to do something nobody had done since the beginning.

He read Curtis Haith's autopsy report. Then he looked at the photographs taken of Crystal Weimer's injuries on the morning of the twenty-seventh of January, two thousand and one.

His conclusion was that her injuries were consistent with her account. The horseplaying with Gibson, a few days before the murder.

⟨HELD⟩

On the twenty-seventh of June, two thousand and sixteen, the charges against Crystal Weimer were dropped with prejudice.

That is the strongest form the ending of a case can take. It means the Commonwealth cannot bring the charge against her again. Ever.

## ENDING (28:00–30:00)

Go back to the first morning.

She told them where she had been. Her family said the same. The blood on her shirt was Gibson's, and the laboratory confirmed it within weeks. Nothing at the scene was hers, and what was there suggested a man.

Fifteen years later, an expert looked at the photographs from that morning and said the marks on her were what she had always said they were.

⟨HELD⟩

Everything in between happened anyway.

Not because someone found something. Nobody ever found anything. A witness came forward twenty months late with a detail that had already been disproved, and then changed his story twice, and then took it back on the stand — and the case went on. A dentist said she could not tell, and a second expert was asked instead, and when the timing did not work the timing was revised without anyone looking at anything new.

⟨HELD⟩

Crystal Weimer spent more than eleven years in prison.

⟨HELD⟩

Curtis Haith was killed on the twenty-seventh of January, two thousand and one. He was twenty-two.

None of what you have heard establishes who did it. The convictions being vacated, the charges being dropped — that resolves one thing only, which is that the case against her was not a case.

His killing has never been answered.
