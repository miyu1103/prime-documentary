## 0. Read this first: the four ways this film goes wrong

**One. It becomes an argument about renewables.** The single most common retelling of this event is a
fight about wind turbines, and it is not what the record says. Freeze-related outages happened across
fuel types, and the largest single share of the fuel problem was **gas that could not reach the
plants** — partly because the grid had cut power to the gas fields (TX-16, TX-17). ⛔-03 governs.
The film never names a villain fuel.

**Two. It says the grid collapsed.** It did not. The grid **shed load to avoid collapsing**, and the
margin is the most quotable number in the whole story: **four minutes and twenty-three seconds against
a nine-minute threshold** (TX-11, TX-12). ⛔-02.

**Three. It uses the dead as texture.** 246 people died and about two-thirds of them froze (TX-26,
TX-27). No victim is named, shown or characterised, no household is invented, no bill belongs to a
face. ⛔-05.

**Four. It ends on "and nothing changed".** Things did change: a state law, a weatherization rule,
a supply-chain map, penalties up to a million dollars a day (TX-37–TX-41). The honest ending is not
that nothing happened; it is that **the thing that was recommended in 2011 was made mandatory only
after 246 people died** — and that is a harder, truer ending than cynicism.

## 1. CONTROLLING IDEA

> **A system that protects itself by cutting a circuit, and finds its own supply on the other end
> of it.**

The grid shed load to save itself. The load it shed included the gas facilities that fuelled it.
Losing that fuel forced it to shed more. That is not a metaphor — it is the sequence the federal
report describes (TX-17, TX-18), and everything else in the film is the ten years and the 1935
decision that arranged it.

**The one-sentence contradiction:**

> Ten years earlier the same thing happened on a smaller scale, a federal report recommended that
> power plants be built to survive the cold, the standard was never made — and when it happened
> again the grid saved itself by cutting power to the gas fields that fuelled it.

## 2. Three problems this film has, and how each becomes structure

| problem | why it is a problem | what it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| The mechanism is electrical and abstract | Frequency, megawatts and load shedding are not pictures | **The film is built on ONE number the audience can hold: four minutes and twenty-three seconds.** Everything technical is explained only as far as that number needs |
| The audience arrives with a political answer | Half of them think they already know whose fault it was | The film **states the political argument out loud in ACT_1 and then declines it**, and spends its evidence on the loop instead. Naming the trap disarms it |
| The villain is a decision not to make a rule | An omission is invisible | **ACT_2 is built entirely on 2011** — the event, the report, the recommendation, and the sentence "NERC ultimately declined to act on that." The absence is dramatised before the disaster happens |

## 5. REGISTER — the voice

Third person. Past tense. Short declaratives. **Zero emotion commands.** No "imagine", no
"unthinkable", no "you won't believe".

Every technical term is defined once, in the sentence that uses it: *load shedding*, *frequency*,
*megawatt*, *critical load*, *interconnection*. Nothing is explained twice and nothing is explained
that the picture already says.

**The film's discipline about blame:** it uses the passive voice exactly where the record does.
"The recommendation was not made a standard" is the sentence, because that is what happened; naming
a person would be an invention.

## 8. THE HOOK — voiced from frame 0, written FIRST

Narration audio starts at **0:00 ± 0.5 s**. No outcome in the first ten seconds. A time, a place, one
person doing one thing, ending on something they do not know.

> **"At twenty past one in the morning, in a control room north of Austin, an operator gave an order
> that had never been given at that scale in American history: cut the power to twenty thousand
> megawatts of customers, right now, before the grid takes itself apart. It was the correct order.
> What nobody in that room could have followed, in the minutes they had, was that the customers
> about to lose their power included the gas fields that fuelled the plants they were trying to
> save."**

Ninety-four words, ~30 s at 191.7 wpm — trim in the edit to land at 0:20 with the last clause intact.

**What the hook may not do:** give a death toll, name a company or an official, use the word
"collapse", or state that the grid failed.

## 10. PRODUCIBILITY — the staging plan, named before the shot list

**This episode was chosen partly because of this section.** It is set in 2021, and 93.5 % of the
shelf's playable clips are modern stock. Four period candidates (1911/1987/1988/1989) were rejected
on 2026-08-21 precisely because the shelf holds 26 clips carrying a title year in 1955–1985.

| register | plan | ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| American suburban street, snow-covered | `small_town_main_street`, `snowy_street_night` — **eyeball: most snow footage is northern-city or Nordic** | 45 cuts |
| Ordinary domestic interior, cold | commissioned plates; the shelf's interiors are staged and warm | 30 cuts |
| Power lines, substations, transmission | `cell_tower_silhouette`, industrial rows — **verify none is European** | 25 cuts |
| Wellheads, gas plant, pipeline | commissioned; the shelf has no oilfield register that survives review | 25 cuts |
| Control room, screens | commissioned; `security_monitors_wall` is a hacking cliché and is barred | 15 cuts |
| Water: taps, treatment, bottled | mixed; commissioned for the treatment plant | 20 cuts |
| Documents, offices, the Capitol | commissioned; blank grounds, typography in Remotion | 30 cuts |

**Three rules that are not negotiable.**

1. **The snow is wrong by default.** A snow query returns Scandinavia, ski resorts and deep
   snowbanks. Texas in Uri was two to six inches on ground with no plough, ice on live oaks and
   palms, a strip-mall car park, a frozen fountain in a warm-climate town. `forbidden_subjects`
   carries fourteen terms for this and **a human still has to open the contact sheet.**
2. **Run `check_cross_episode_reuse.py` before staging.** EP72 has just spent this shelf's
   night-road, rail and emergency-light registers.
3. **No AI-motion drift.** The channel's normal shape is ~68 % archive; EP62–65 drifted to 44–57 %
   AI motion and that is recorded as a regression.

## 12. WHAT THIS FILM MUST NOT SAY

The full list is `⛔-01…⛔-09` in the ledger. The four that will be tempting:

1. **"The wind turbines froze and that caused it."** Barred. ⛔-03
2. **"The grid collapsed."** Barred. It shed load to avoid collapse. ⛔-02
3. **"Winterization was required and they ignored it."** Barred. It was **recommended** and **not
   made a standard**. ⛔-01
4. **"Seven hundred people really died."** Barred as fact. The state's final figure is 246; other
   estimates are attributed and labelled. ⛔-04
