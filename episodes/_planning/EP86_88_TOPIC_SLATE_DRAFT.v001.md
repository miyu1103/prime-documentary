## 2. CANDIDATE DETAIL

### 1. Columbia — *"NASA Cancelled the Photo Request. Columbia Flew 16 More Days."*
- **Hook (1行):** Engineers watched the foam hit the wing on launch video. They asked three times for spy-satellite photos of the damage. Management cancelled the request 90 minutes after it reached the Pentagon.
- **Contradiction:** The agency that could see everything chose not to look — because if the answer was bad, there was (they believed) nothing they could do.
- **Ordinary person:** Rodney Rocha, chief structural engineer, whose e-mail asking whether the crew had been told to inspect the wing went unanswered — and the 7 crew, who spent 16 days doing science over a hole in the wing.
- **System:** NASA's Mission Management Team; "normalized deviance" — foam strikes had happened on 65 prior flights and become paperwork.
- **Primary sources:** CAIB Report (public, exhaustive, quotable); NASA's own case study on Rocha (appel.nasa.gov PDF); all NASA footage public domain — **the register problem does not exist for this film**.
- **R-class:** R2 (all facts federal-record; crew named respectfully, no re-created final moments).
- **Novelty:** No space story in EP1–85. Zero slug collision.
- **Demand:** median 577,633, 13 channels ≥100k, R-36 PASS (batch2, 2026-08-20). US 55+ watched it live on a Saturday morning.

### 2. El Faro — *"The Ship Sailed Into a Hurricane. The Company's Weather Data Was Six Hours Old."*
- **Hook:** 33 American crew; the captain steered toward Joaquin using outdated forecast files while junior officers' suggestions to divert went unheeded; the bridge audio of the last 26 hours was recovered from 15,000 feet down (○ re-verify durations against the NTSB VDR transcript before scripting).
- **Contradiction:** Everyone on the bridge could see the storm. The one man with authority to turn was certain, and the system gave nobody else a voice — and the Coast Guard's own report then said it would have pursued the dead captain's licence, while seeking **no criminal penalty from anyone alive**.
- **Ordinary person:** the junior officers on the night watch, on the recording, deferring.
- **System:** TOTE Maritime (safety officer position left unfilled, crew-rest violations), the ABS inspection regime, and the USCG's own oversight — the Marine Board names all three plus the captain.
- **Primary sources:** NTSB accident report + published VDR bridge transcript (the richest primary audio-record of any candidate on this board); USCG Marine Board of Investigation final report.
- **R-class:** R2 (all deceased; federal record; dignity rules on the final audio — quote sparingly).
- **Novelty:** distinct from concordia (EP80: passenger ship, convicted captain) and titan (EP16). Zero slug collision.
- **Demand:** median 399,302, 7 channels ≥100k, PASS (measured 2026-09-05). Shelf: `ocean waves coast storm` register = 145 clips (best-stocked disaster register we have, per TOPIC_POOL_500).
- **Honest negative:** Brick Immortar's 73-min film (4.75M) is a definitive incumbent — but it is also exactly the external-feeder mechanism (§4 of direction v002) that carried Titan and Cooper.
