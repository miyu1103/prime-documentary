## EP78 `fukushima` — **the premise is dead as written**

| what the slate said | what the record says |
|---|---|
| "Three executives were **acquitted of criminal negligence**, then in 2022 a civil court ordered them to pay **¥13 trillion**" | Both halves happened. **And then the second half was taken away.** |

- **July 2022** — Tokyo District Court ordered former chairman **Tsunehisa Katsumata**, former
  president **Masataka Shimizu** and former executive vice-presidents **Ichiro Takekuro** and
  **Sakae Muto** to pay **¥13,321 billion** to the company. `[SECONDARY: JURIST, CNIC, D&O Diary]`
- The shareholders had sued in **March 2012**, seeking about **¥23 trillion** from five
  executives. `[SECONDARY]`
- **6 June 2025 — the Tokyo High Court overturned it.** Presiding judge **Toshikazu Kino**
  found the executives could not have predicted the tsunami. `[SECONDARY: JURIST, Nippon.com]`
- **March 2025 — the criminal acquittal became final.** `[SECONDARY: Japan Times headline,
  Nippon.com]`

**So the film the slate imagined cannot be made.** There is no "acquitted, then made to pay".

**What is left is a different and colder story:** the worst nuclear accident since Chernobyl,
a Diet investigation commission that in 2012 called it a **manmade disaster**, and — after
thirteen years of litigation — **no individual held liable in any forum.** `[SECONDARY]`

**That is a real PD premise and it is not the one that was greenlit.** It has no accountability
payoff, and the channel's own direction requires that fear land on preparedness rather than
despair. **This is an owner decision, and three options are set out at the end of this file.**

## EP79 `alaska261` — **stronger than the slate, with one hole**

- **NTSB probable cause:** loss of pitch control from in-flight failure of the horizontal
  stabiliser trim system **jackscrew assembly's acme nut threads**, caused by excessive wear
  from **insufficient lubrication by Alaska Airlines**. `[NTSB AAR-02/01]`
- **The regulator did not approve one extension. It approved four.** Alaska Airlines received
  FAA permission to extend the jackscrew lubrication interval **four times between 1987 and
  1996** — from an original **500 flight hours** to, by July 1996, **8 calendar months**, about
  **2,550 flight hours**. `[SECONDARY: Seattle Times reporting of the NTSB; confirm against
  AAR-02/01 before scripting]`
- The NTSB found the extended interval **and the FAA's approval of it** increased the
  likelihood that a missed or inadequate lubrication would cause excessive wear. `[NTSB]`

**A five-fold extension, granted four times by the agency whose job is to say no.** That is a
better spine than the slate had.

**The hole:** civil only. All but one of **88 wrongful-death suits** settled, with **Alaska
conceding liability** and Boeing agreeing not to contest it — and **no criminal charges were
brought**. `[SECONDARY]` The slate promised "a court at the centre"; here the court is a
settlement table and a declination. **Weaker on that criterion than 77, 80 or 82.**

## EP80 `concordia` — **confirmed, and final**

- Convicted **11 February 2015** after a 19-month trial: manslaughter, causing a maritime
  accident, and abandoning ship. **Sixteen years** — ten for manslaughter, five for causing the
  shipwreck, one for abandoning passengers. `[SECONDARY]`
- Appeal court upheld it **31 May 2016**. `[SECONDARY]`
- **The Court of Cassation, Italy's highest criminal court, upheld conviction and sentence on
  12 May 2017.** He began serving at Rebibbia. `[SECONDARY: VOA, CBS, CBC]`

**Nothing in the slate needs changing.** Get the Cassation judgment itself before scripting.

## EP81 `station` — **a different story from the one in the slate**

The slate said "the owners and the tour manager were **criminally convicted**". The record is
more specific and, for a film, better:

- In **2006** the Derderians resolved the charges by **pleading no contest to 100 counts each
  of involuntary manslaughter**. A no-contest plea is not a trial conviction. `[SECONDARY]`
- **Michael Derderian: four years in prison.** **Jeffrey Derderian: no prison** — a suspended
  ten-year sentence, three years' probation and 500 hours of community service. `[SECONDARY]`
- The consolidated civil settlement was finalised at about **$176 million**. `[SECONDARY:
  FindLaw]` A separate **$1 million** settlement was reached with members of the band whose
  pyrotechnics started the fire on **20 February 2003**. `[SECONDARY]`
- The claims described a club where **an exit door swung the wrong way**, overcrowding was
  permitted, and **foam that experts said burned like gasoline** lined the walls by the stage.
  `[SECONDARY]`

**One hundred people died. One brother served four years and the other served none.** That is
the contradiction, and it is sharper than "the owners were convicted".

**Careful:** the tour manager is a separate defendant with a separate outcome and was **not**
verified in this pass. Do not name him or his sentence until he is.

## EP82 `valdez` — **confirmed, and the arithmetic is better than the slate said**

- **1994** — a jury awarded **$5 billion** in punitive damages. `[SECONDARY]`
- On appeal the award was **cut to $2.5 billion**. `[SECONDARY]`
- **25 June 2008** — the Supreme Court, in **Exxon Shipping Co. v. Baker**, reduced it to about
  **$500 million**, anchoring punitive damages to the **$507.5 million** compensatory figure at
  roughly one to one. `[SECONDARY: Washington Legal Foundation, plaintiff-side and defence-side
  case notes; get the opinion itself]`

**From $5 billion to about $500 million, and nineteen years from the spill to the ruling.**
The slate said "cut them nineteen years later"; the record supports it and adds the ratio rule
that made it happen, which is the system this episode should anatomise.

**Not verified:** the slate's draft title claims the spill plan promised barges in **five
hours** and that fourteen passed. **No source for either number was found in this pass.** Do
not use that title until it is sourced or it becomes the next "four blackouts before departure".

## The EP78 decision, for the owner

**Option A — make it as the story it now is.** *The worst nuclear accident in a generation, a
Diet commission calling it manmade, and thirteen years of litigation ending with no individual
liable anywhere.* Keeps the highest measured demand of the whole slate (median 958,264, ten
channels above 100k). Costs the accountability payoff; the ending is bleak, and PD's own
direction warns that unrelieved dread exhausts an audience.

**Option B — replace EP78 from the reserves.** In order of measured demand: **Colgan Air 3407**
(232,564 — American, and a change in the law came out of it), **Triangle Shirtwaist 1911**
(114,926 — and **no long film exists anywhere**, the emptiest slot in 73 probed queries),
**MGM Grand 1980** (98,385).

**Option C — keep Fukushima but move it to the end of the slate**, and use the time to check
whether the shareholders have appealed the June 2025 reversal to the Supreme Court of Japan.
**If that appeal is live, the story becomes "and it is still not over", which is a different
and better ending.** That was not established in this pass.

**Recommendation: B, with Colgan Air 3407.** American, a court and a statute at the centre, and
it does not ask the audience to sit through thirteen years of litigation that ends in nothing.

## What this pass cost, and what it saved

Six searches and two fetches. Against that: **one script written on a dead premise would have
been about five thousand words, three hours of narration and render, and a film whose central

this session; the only reason it did not reach a render is that the record was opened first.

**Do this before every episode from now on.** The slate is chosen on demand. **The premise is
verified on the record.** They are not the same job.
