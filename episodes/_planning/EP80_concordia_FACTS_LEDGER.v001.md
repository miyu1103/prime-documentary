>

> film may say so **only** from a court source that is in the Sources table below and marked as

>
> **A safety investigation is not a trial and says so about itself.** Its purpose is to prevent
> the next casualty, not to apportion blame or liability. Every line taken from it must be used
> the way the Body meant it — as a finding about a system — and never as a verdict about a person.

> report finds a bridge team that warned nobody, a general emergency alarm that was never
> effectively given, an abandon-ship order 69 minutes late, and nearly two thousand passengers
> who had never attended a muster. Those are the film.
>

> **Thirty-two people died and two were never found.** No bodies, no funerals, no grieving

## 1 · The ship and the people

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| CC-001 | The Costa Concordia was on a weekly Mediterranean cruise. The casualty happened off Giglio Island in the Tyrrhenian Sea on **13 January 2012**; the event is classified in the report as **contact, breach, black out** | 17 |
| CC-002 | There were **4,229 people on board: 3,206 passengers and 1,023 crew** | 8 |
| CC-003 | Sea and weather were **favourable** | 8 |
| CC-004 | **Only 1,270 of the 3,206 passengers had attended a muster of passengers** — those who had embarked at Savona. **The rest received safety instructions by video** | 90 |
| CC-005 | Of those, 463 had received the video instructions at Toulon, 456 at Barcelona, and further groups elsewhere — because passengers joined at successive ports through the week | 90 |
| CC-006 | The crew are required to drill on a leak emergency every six months. Crew interviews put the last such drill at the end of the previous year, but **the records were lost in the casualty, so there is no evidence either way** | 90 |

**CC-004 is the fact this film is built on.** Nearly two thousand people aboard had never stood
at a muster station.

## 3 · What the Body concluded

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| CC-101 | The report states that **"the human element is the root cause in the Costa Concordia casualty"** — in the unconventional action that caused the contact, and in the emergency management afterwards | 14 |
| CC-102 | On the bridge team: **"Nobody seemed to have urged the Master to accelerate the turn or to give warning on the looming danger."** The Body says the accident may lead to a wider discussion of the adequacy of bridge teams | 11 |
| CC-103 | **"The General Emergency Alarm was not activated immediately after the impact."** This delayed the management of everything that followed — the flooding and the abandon ship process | 11 |
| CC-104 | Analysis of crew certification, the muster list and familiarisation training found **inconsistencies in the assignment of duties to some crew members** | 11 |
| CC-105 | There was a **lack of direct orders from the bridge** to the crew involved, leaving initiatives to individuals. Different backgrounds and basic training among the crew may have played a part | 11 |
| CC-106 | On the evacuation: passengers were not directed to muster stations according to procedure. **"There was chaos and confusion, lack of communication; in other words a complete disorganization, mainly because nobody by the bridge coordinated the emergency."** | 88 |
| CC-107 | The same passage records the other half. Passengers testified that **despite the chaos, crew members supported them with humanity and effective action**, and ferried them to the boats and off the ship | 88 |
| CC-108 | The flooding was immediate and irreversible. **Two contiguous watertight compartments, 5 and 6, were violently flooded**; compartment 5 filled completely in a very short time | 12 |
| CC-109 | The Body states plainly that no measure may be **"sufficient to render the ship unsinkable when more than two contiguous watertight compartments are flooded"** | 8 |

## 4 · The counterfactual the Body ran

| id | fact | page |
|---|---|---|
| CC-201 | The Body modelled what would have happened if the general emergency signal had been given at the second grounding at **22:39**, with passengers gathered and ready | 92 |
| CC-202 | On that timeline the abandon ship would have been launched **36 minutes** after the general emergency signal, which the Body calls adequate to gather everybody in the ship's large and suitable muster stations | 92 |
| CC-203 | Passengers would have begun leaving while the ship had **13 degrees** of starboard heel | 92 |
| CC-204 | **After 33 more minutes, when the ship reached 30 degrees of heel at 23:12, all of them could have reached safety** — from the same position, with the ship already stopped, where 32 people instead died | 92 |
| CC-205 | The Body's account of how those people died: **most were trapped, and the others were thrown into the sea** | 92 |

**CC-204 is the hardest sentence in the report.** The official investigators wrote down that
everyone could have got off.
