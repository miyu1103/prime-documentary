In February 2021 a winter storm came down through the middle of the country. In Texas it got cold, and it stayed cold, for days, across the entire state at once. The Texas Department of State Health Services counted the dead six times, each count larger than the one before. The sixth and final report, in January of twenty twenty-two, put it at two hundred and forty-six.

The cause is on the record. The federal inquiry into February twenty twenty-one made twenty-eight formal recommendations, among them revisions to the reliability standards on generator winterization, and on gas-electric coordination. It found that generating units failed from freezing above their own stated ambient design temperature. Not below it. Above it. No individual was ever established as the cause of it.

This film is about a recommendation that was written down and not made a rule. In two thousand and eleven, Texas got weather like this. Rolling blackouts were ordered, and the federal energy regulator and the organisation that writes American grid reliability standards produced a report. It recommended the development of winterization standards. The standards organisation ultimately declined to act on it. What did happen instead was reminders, every year, through regional workshops. Ten years passed between the report and the storm.

At twenty past one on the morning of the fifteenth, the grid operator ordered load shedding. Twenty thousand megawatts were shed. It was the largest manually controlled load shedding event in United States history. The frequency of the Texas grid fell below fifty-nine point four hertz, and stayed there for four minutes and twenty-three seconds. The line was nine minutes.

Most natural gas production and processing facilities in Texas were not identified as critical load. So when the operator shed twenty thousand megawatts to save the grid, some of what went dark was the gas industry that fuelled it. Forty-three point three per cent of the fuel supply problem was caused by freezing temperatures and weather; twenty-one point five per cent by power losses at midstream, wellhead or gathering facilities.

More than four and a half million people lost power, some of them for four days. One thousand one hundred and five boil-water notices were issued, covering about fourteen and a half million people. Of the roughly half of the state that lost running water altogether, the average outage was fifty-two hours. The wholesale price of electricity was set at the system-wide cap, nine thousand dollars per megawatt-hour, and it ran for days. Some households received bills of seventeen thousand dollars.

Every factual line in the narration is sourced to one of those. Some analysts believe the true figure for the dead is much higher; the film uses the state's number, because it is the state's number, and says plainly that the question is not settled. It gives no single figure for the total cost: the estimates are not reconciled. Whether winterizing to the twenty eleven recommendation would have stopped this storm is not a finding anywhere in the record. The report recommends standards. It does not run the counterfactual, and neither does the film.

ON THE RULES, AS OF AUGUST 2026
Senate Bill Three required the state's oil and gas regulator to make rules requiring critical facilities to weatherize their equipment. Not power stations only, but the gas supply chain that feeds them. A committee now identifies the critical infrastructure and maps the state's electricity supply chain, at least once a year. Penalties run from five thousand dollars per violation per day to one million dollars per violation per day. The rules were adopted on the thirtieth of August, twenty twenty-two. Eleven years, five months and nineteen days after the report that recommended them. On the pricing, an appeals court held that the regulator had set prices too high; the state's supreme court subsequently affirmed the regulator's orders.

ABOUT THE IMAGES
This film uses AI-assisted visualizations. They are symbolic reconstructions, not photographs of the event, they are labelled on screen where they depict the days themselves, and they contain no likeness of any real person. No victim is named, shown or described anywhere in this film.
